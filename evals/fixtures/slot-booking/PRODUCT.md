# Dunmore recycling centre: book a drop-off slot

Read: for residents of Dunmore district who need to bring bulky or sorted waste to the council's recycling centre; the main job is booking a 20-minute drop-off slot for a car, car and trailer or van; tone referent is the site attendant at the gate who checks the booking and points you to the right bay. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]

Contents: Product summary · Users and groups · Top tasks · Context of use · Positioning · Constraints · Brand commitments · Facts · Principles · Accessibility needs · Platforms and locales · Target assurance level · Surfaces

## Product summary

The booking service for Dunmore recycling centre on Quarry Road, run by Dunmore District Council. Since the centre moved to booked slots, residents choose a day and a 20-minute slot, say what vehicle they are bringing and what kind of waste, and get a reference to show at the gate. Nothing is paid; the site was delivered by an agency as a built bundle, and the council's web team keeps only that bundle. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
What makes it different from a shop is that the slot is the scarce thing: a slot holds one vehicle, the centre takes about 120 vehicles a day, and a resident who arrives without a booking, or with the wrong vehicle for the booking, is turned away at the gate. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]

## Context of use

- Devices: mostly phones, the evening before or the morning of the visit; some desktop use. [inferred: gate attendants' notes]
- Scene: a loaded car on the drive, a slot wanted today or tomorrow, often while doing something else. [inferred: gate attendants' notes]
- Frequency: a few times a year per household; more in spring and after a house move. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Connectivity: home broadband and mobile data; the centre itself has poor signal, so the reference is read at the gate from the phone's screen or on paper. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Assistive technology: many residents are older; larger text and dark mode on phones are common; some book with a keyboard only. [inferred: gate attendants' notes]

## Positioning

- Category: council household waste services. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Alternatives: a private skip; a bulky-waste collection from the council; a neighbour's trailer. [inferred: gate attendants' notes]
- Distinctive and true: free to residents, a slot of your own, no queue at the gate when the booking is right. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]

## Constraints

- Technical: static pages served from the council's site; the booking in progress is kept in the browser for the visit; the reference travels in the address between steps; personal details are never put in an address. The agency delivered a built bundle (minified script and stylesheet); no source is available to the web team. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Legal: collect only what the gate needs (a registration number, a name, a mobile number for a reminder, a postcode to confirm the district). [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Brand: the council's name and its green; no mandated typeface. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]

## Brand commitments

- Plain language; the resident is "you"; the rules are stated where they apply, not in a separate page. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Honest about scarcity: a slot shown as free is free at that moment, and a slot that is gone says so. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
- Tone referent from the owner: the gate attendant, because residents trust the person who waves them in and says which bay. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]

## Principles

1. A resident should be able to book on a phone in a few minutes, with every rule shown where it applies. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
2. The gate decides on the reference and the vehicle, so what the booking records must be what the resident meant. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
3. Say what is free now, and say when something is gone. [confirmed: fixture brief, 2026-10-12]
