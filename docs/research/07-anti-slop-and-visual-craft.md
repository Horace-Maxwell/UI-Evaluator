# 07 — Anti-slop and visual craft: what makes UI look AI-generated, and the craft knowledge that prevents it

- Date: 2026-10-01
- ID prefix for adopted items: `CRAFT-`
- Status: complete first pass (research note; evidence base for the CRAFT-* rules)

## 1. Scope and method

**Scope.** (A) What makes a UI read as AI-generated or generic in 2024–2026, with detection methods, remedies and legitimate exceptions; (B) the visual-craft knowledge, with concrete numbers, that prevents it — typography, colour, spacing/layout, hierarchy, depth, radius, icons, imagery, motion, interaction polish, microcopy, and Refactoring UI's principles; (C) how to make "taste" operational for a coding agent — direction-setting from brand attributes, reference use and its licensing, rubrics, and computational aesthetics (AIM, UIClip and related metrics with validity evidence). Out of scope here (covered by sibling notes): heuristic evaluation and severity scales, usability testing, A/B testing, full WCAG auditing.

**Method.** Primary sources first; repositories were shallow-cloned and the actual files read; web pages were fetched directly (or through a text-rendering proxy for JS-only pages); X/Twitter posts were verified through the public syndication endpoint; Hacker News threads through the Algolia API. Copyrighted books (Refactoring UI, Bringhurst) were not reproduced — Refactoring UI is paraphrased from its public table of contents and the authors' widely known principles; Bringhurst's numbers are cited only through sources that quote them. Every number carries its source; anything not verified is marked **[unverified]**; proposals from this synthesis are marked **[HEUR]**; inferences are marked **[inference]**.

## 2. Part A — The tells (catalog of AI-generated / generic UI signals)

### 2.1 Why generated UIs converge (mechanisms and the quality of the evidence)

| Mechanism | What it says | Evidence quality | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distributional convergence | Sampling fills every unspecified design decision with the high-probability centre of the training data; "safe" choices dominate web data, so an unguided model lands on them. | Practitioner/vendor explanation; consistent with how sampling works, not measured on UI directly. | Anthropic blog "Improving frontend design through Skills" (Rajasekaran, Wei, Bricken, 2025-11-12) https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills ; OpenAI "Designing delightful frontends with GPT-5.4" (dated March 2026 by gstack; undated page) https://developers.openai.com/blog/designing-delightful-frontends-with-gpt-5-4 |
| Toolchain defaults | Generators share one narrow stack (Tailwind, shadcn/ui, Lucide, Framer Motion, Geist via `next/font`); its demo defaults are emitted verbatim. Canonical case: Tailwind UI's `bg-indigo-500` buttons. Adam Wathan (Tailwind creator) publicly "apologized" for it on 2025-08-07 (22k likes), quoting a post that GPT-5 had not solved "the purple problem". | Strong anecdotal + mechanism; the specific causal share is unmeasured. | https://x.com/adamwathan/status/1953510802159219096 (text and date verified via the public syndication endpoint); signs-of-ai-design README https://github.com/febbhav/signs-of-ai-design |
| Feedback loop | Generated pages ship, get indexed, re-enter training data, and concentrate the bias. | Plausible; direct UI evidence absent. An analogous peer-reviewed result exists for images: autonomous SDXL↔LLaVA text→image→text loops converged on 12 generic motifs across 700 trajectories ("visual elevator music"). | Hintze, *Patterns* (Cell), 2025-12-19, DOI 10.1016/j.patter.2025.101451, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12827715/ |
| Preference tuning | Aesthetic-rating tuning rewards centred, saturated, symmetric, flattering output and removes "boring" regions. | Stated for image models; for UI it is a hypothesis. [unverified for UI] | signs-of-ai-design "Why models converge" |
| The anti-slop advice itself becomes slop | Any fixed recommendation, repeated across users, becomes the next default. Documented first-hand: Anthropic's Nov-2025 blog post and cookbook notebook recommended Space Grotesk, Fraunces, Playfair Display, Bricolage Grotesque, Newsreader, Clash Display, IBM Plex and Crimson Pro, and the v1 skill recommended noise textures, grain overlays, gradient meshes and staggered page-load reveals; by mid-2026 those exact faces and devices appear on detector blacklists (impeccable, gstack, Krebs), and Anthropic's own skill now lists "cream + high-contrast serif + terracotta" (its own brand accent `#D97757`) as tell #1. The unslop-ui skill author states that the first version of their skill recommended cream + forest green + Fraunces, which became recognisably "AI-tasteful". | Strong (primary documents, dated commits). | anthropics/skills commits `00756142ab` (2025-12-04), `2235be7c60` (2026-06-09), `41bbe19d1a` (2026-09-03, PR #1713); claude-cookbooks `coding/prompting_for_frontend_aesthetics.ipynb`; https://github.com/JCarterJohnson/vibecoded-design-tells `skill/references/choosing-a-look.md` |

Practical consequence for UI-Evaluator: the tell catalogue must be **versioned data with dates and a decay status**, not prose baked into the skill, and the generation guidance must prescribe a *decision method* rather than a *list of good fonts/palettes* (see §5 conflicts C1–C3).

### 2.2 Source notes (what was read, what each contributes)

**Anthropic `frontend-design` skill** (Apache-2.0; `skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` in https://github.com/anthropics/skills). Three versions were read via the commit history:
- *v1 (Nov–Dec 2025)*: commit to a BOLD tone from a list of extremes; avoid Inter/Roboto/Arial/system fonts and "purple gradients on white"; recommends gradient meshes, noise, grain, dramatic shadows, staggered page-load reveals; warns the model still converges on Space Grotesk.
- *v2 (2026-06-09)*: reframes as a studio design lead; adds grounding in the subject's own world, the idea that structural devices must encode information (numbered markers only for real sequences), names three converged looks (cream + serif + terracotta; near-black + acid-green/vermilion; broadsheet hairlines/zero radius); two-pass process (token plan → review against brief → build); quality floor; Chanel's advice to take off one accessory before leaving; a copywriting section.
- *v3 (2026-09-03, PR #1713)*: adds two more clusters — the **SaaS-card kit** (identical rounded cards, one radius everywhere, the same `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadow, gradient washes) and **template chrome** (tracked ALL-CAPS eyebrow over every heading, `A · B · C` meta strings, `WORD — fragment` labels, tinted near-black `#0B0B0B/#111` for black, monospace for small data labels, `→` appended to links/buttons); typographic tells (one accented word in a headline; all-caps labels; unnecessary labels above content); line length < 80 characters, serif body slightly more leading; motion: fade-and-slide-up entrances on each section and hover transitions on every card are named as generic defaults that look generated; plan colour as 4–6 named hex values; concentrate boldness in a single place.
- The blog post (2025-11-12) gives pairing heuristics of extreme weight contrast (100–200 against 800–900 rather than 400 against 600) and size jumps of three times or more rather than 1.5×. These are prompt heuristics for escaping blandness, not typographic rules (a 3× jump is wrong for app UI; see §3.1).

**OpenAI, "Designing delightful frontends with GPT-5.4"** (https://developers.openai.com/blog/designing-delightful-frontends-with-gpt-5-4; the page shows no date — gstack's source comment dates it March 2026). Landing-page doctrine: first viewport is one composition; a "hero budget" (brand, one headline, one supporting sentence, one CTA group, one dominant image); default no cards, never cards in the hero; max two typefaces; one accent colour; no default purple-on-white and no bias toward purple or toward dark mode; imagery must show product/place/context; 2–3 intentional motions for visually-led work; delete copy while deleting 30% improves it. Seven **hard rejections** (generic SaaS card grid first impression; beautiful image with weak brand; strong headline with no clear action; busy imagery behind text; sections repeating the same mood statement; carousel with no narrative purpose; app UI made of stacked cards) and seven **litmus checks** (brand unmistakable in first screen; one strong visual anchor; understandable by scanning headlines only; one job per section; are cards necessary; does motion improve hierarchy; would it still feel premium with decorative shadows removed). Workflow: define system first, mood boards/references, Playwright screenshot inspection across viewports.

**impeccable** (Paul Bakaus; Apache-2.0; v4.1.0, engine 0.1.10; https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable, read at commit `4adabaf`, 2026-10-01). The most rigorous open detector: **61 deterministic rules (32 "slop", 29 "quality")** in `crates/live/assets/antipatterns.json`, implemented in Rust (`crates/detect/src/regex_matchers.rs`, `crates/core/src/checks/*.rs`, `crates/core/src/browser/*.rs`) over both source text and a live DOM. Thresholds used below were read from the code. Other contributions: four visitor *modes* (Persuade / Operate / Read / Experience) that change which rules apply; a "craft floor" (`skill/reference/craft-floor.md`) with Verify/Refuse lists and **per-model sections** (`<codex>` ghost cards, over-rounding, sketchy SVG, stripes, grid backgrounds, "X theater" copy; `<gemini>` image-hover; `<claude>` cream/serif/lamplight prior); a "design specificity verdict" (could an unrelated product use this unchanged?) taken *before* reading detector output; dual isolated assessments (LLM review and detector) to avoid anchoring; Nielsen 0–4 scoring with n/a renormalisation; a `concept-seed` randomiser that deals candidate directions so runs do not converge.

**gstack** (Garry Tan; MIT; https://github.com/garrytan/gstack at `7fca42a`). `lib/design-catalog.ts` is a typed catalogue derived in part from impeccable (NOTICE.md documents the Apache-2.0 derivation): each entry has `detect` (engine/grep/render/llm), `confidence` (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), review tier (auto-fix/ask/possible) and impact. `review/design-checklist.md` gives grep heuristics with numeric thresholds (e.g. >60% of text containers centred; >80% of radii share one value ≥16px; `backdrop-filter` on >1 container). `design-review/SKILL.md` adds a ~80-item, 10-category audit, a first-impression protocol ("the first 3 things my eye goes to are…"), a trunk test, and an A–F grading (each high-impact finding −1 letter, medium −½) with a separate **AI Slop Score**. It also embeds OpenAI's hard rejections and litmus checks.

**Hallmark** (Nutlope; MIT; https://github.com/Nutlope/hallmark, v1.1.0). 58 "slop gates" (`skills/hallmark/references/slop-test.md`) and a **pre-emit self-critique on six axes** (Philosophy, Hierarchy, Execution, Specificity, Restraint, Variety; any score < 3 forces a revision). Distinctive emphases: *structural* variety across outputs (a project log `.hallmark/log.json` so successive builds do not share a macrostructure), "honest copy" (no invented metrics, gate 46), no re-drawn browser/phone/terminal chrome (gate 47), locked tokens (gate 48), mobile at 320/375/414/768 px, all eight interactive states.

**taste-skill** (Leonxlnx; MIT; https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill). Three dials (DESIGN_VARIANCE, MOTION_INTENSITY, VISUAL_DENSITY, baseline 8/6/4) inferred from a one-line "design read"; long lists of "production-test tells" (version labels in hero, section-number eyebrows, middle-dot strips, decorative status dots, fake product UI built from divs, poetic section labels like "Field notes", locale/weather strips, scroll cues). Several rules are strong opinions without evidence (a total em-dash ban; strong discouragement of serifs as a default; recommending Geist/Outfit/Satoshi, which other sources list as overused; recommending deliberately irregular fake numbers) — see conflicts in §5.

**Other skills/guidelines read**: ibelick `ui-skills` (MIT; `baseline-ui`: no gradients unless asked, no purple/multicolour gradients, no glow as primary affordance, ≤200 ms interaction feedback, one accent per view; `improve-ui`: a finding needs contract + runtime + correction proof, report at most three). Vercel `web-interface-guidelines` (MIT; https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines; interaction/animation/layout/design rules used in Part B). `ui-ux-pro-max-skill` (MIT; 132k stars) maps ~190 product categories to styles, palettes and font pairings (e.g. "Playfair Display + Inter" for luxury) — a category→aesthetic lookup, which is precisely what impeccable/gstack treat as the failure mode: if the look can be guessed from the category alone, the run should start over.

### 2.3 Model- and era-specific priors (the target moves)

- **2024–mid 2025 (first order)**: indigo/violet gradients, Inter, centred hero + three icon cards, untouched shadcn, glassmorphism, emoji icons (Wathan tweet; Krebs; Reddit ranking).
- **Late 2025–2026 (second order)**: warm cream ground + high-contrast/italic serif + terracotta/clay (explicitly the *Claude* prior in impeccable's `<claude>` block and Anthropic's own calibration list); near-black + one neon accent + glow ("Wargames/retro-terminal" look — HN "Ask HN: What's with the Wargames-like UX lately?" 2026-04-15 https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47774003); broadsheet hairlines + tiny tracked mono labels; emerald as the "purple-escape" accent.
- **Model fingerprints** (impeccable craft-floor; signs-of-ai-design code-level section): GPT/Codex — hairline border + wide soft shadow ("ghost card"), over-rounded cards, sketchy SVG doodles and `feTurbulence` grain, repeating stripes, grid-paper backgrounds, "X theater" phrasing; Gemini — hover-animated images, Material Symbols icons; v0 — Geist + shadcn `@/components/ui/*`; Lovable/Bolt — generator meta tags / attribution.
- Implication: an evaluator must (a) ship the catalogue with `first_seen`/`status` fields and (b) also run *generic* checks that do not depend on today's fashion: "was this decision made for this brief?", stacking of defaults, cross-output similarity.

### 2.4 Catalogue of tells

Conventions. *Detect* uses: **SRC** = regex/AST over source; **DOM** = computed style/geometry in a rendered page; **PIX** = screenshot/visual model; **COPY** = text analysis; **JDG** = human/LLM judgement against the brief. *Rel.* = single-instance reliability (S strong / M moderate / W weak) adapted from signs-of-ai-design and corroborated by Krebs/Reddit data where available. Thresholds are quoted from the cited implementation, not invented here. "Acceptable when" records the legitimate contexts — almost every tell is a *default*, not an *error*.

### 2.5 How to use the catalogue without producing false accusations or new slop

1. **Stack, don't single-shot.** Krebs grades sites by count (0–1 low, 2–3 medium, 4+ high); signs-of-ai-design's rule is "one match is noise, ten is a signature". UI-Evaluator should report a *density* score plus the list, never "this is AI".
2. **Judge the absence of decisions, not the presence of a style.** Each tell is acceptable when the brief or the documented design system asks for it ("the brief's own words always win" — Anthropic v2/v3; impeccable "the brief wins"; gstack suppresses patterns blessed in DESIGN.md).
3. **Severity by audience**: P0 a layperson notices, P1 a designer notices, P2 craft (avoid-ai-design). Quality defects (contrast, hidden content, overflow, missing states) are not "taste" and should be fixed regardless.
4. **Specificity first, detector second**: make the unrelated-product judgement (could another product use this unchanged?) *before* reading detector output to avoid anchoring (impeccable critique: isolated assessments A and B).
5. **Version the list**: each tell carries `first_seen`, `status` (active/rising/migrating/obsolete), and `evidence` (Krebs prevalence, Reddit perception, practitioner consensus).

## 3. Part B — Visual craft knowledge with concrete numbers

Evidence labels used in this part: **[STD]** normative standard (WCAG); **[PLAT]** platform design system values (Apple HIG, Material 3 tokens, Carbon); **[EMP]** empirical study; **[PRAC]** respected practitioner guidance; **[HEUR]** heuristic proposed here from the synthesis. Numbers from platform systems are *calibrations*, not laws: they show where mature systems landed.

### 3.1 Typography

**Choosing and pairing faces (process).**
- Choose for a stated reason tied to the subject and the surface mode; use one family or two clearly different ones (Anthropic v3; OpenAI: max two faces without justification; Hallmark: 2 + at most one "outlier" face in ≤2 slots) [PRAC].
- Operate/Read surfaces are well served by a workhorse UI face or the system stack; Persuade/Experience surfaces want a face with a point of view (impeccable `new-work.md`) [PRAC]. Material 3 formalises the same split: a "brand" face for Display/Headline and a "plain" face for Body/Label (https://m3.material.io/styles/typography/type-scale-tokens) [PLAT].
- Verify before use: exact family name, required weights, **license** and loading URL from the official listing; omit faces you cannot verify (gstack `design-consultation` font procedure) [PRAC]. Several "distinctive" faces recommended by community skills (GT Sectra, Tiempos, Söhne, Canela, PP Editorial New in taste-skill) are commercial and cannot be shipped without a licence; impeccable's font matcher ranks candidates from a fingerprint index of 3,076 Google Fonts entries (`skill/scripts/data/font-index.json`, `crates/comp/src/font_index.rs`), i.e. openly licensed faces that can actually ship.
- Avoid Ultralight/Thin/Light weights for small text; if a custom face is thin, go larger than the platform minimums (Apple HIG Typography, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) [PLAT].
- Check language coverage (CJK, accents), the presence of tabular figures and true small caps, and an optical-size axis if available; test with the real copy at every breakpoint (impeccable craft floor) [PRAC].

**Body size floors.**

| Source | Value |
|---|---|
| Butterick, *Practical Typography* (summary of key rules) | 15–25 px body on the web; 10–12 pt print — https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html [PRAC] |
| Apple HIG | iOS/iPadOS default 17 pt, minimum 11 pt; macOS 13/10; visionOS 17/12; watchOS 16/12 [PLAT] |
| Material 3 tokens | Body Large 16/24, Body Medium 14/20, Body Small 12/16, Label Small 11/16 (`material-web/tokens/versions/latest/sass/_md-sys-typescale.scss`) [PLAT] |
| Vercel guidelines | mobile `<input>` font-size ≥16 px to avoid iOS zoom [PRAC] |
| impeccable detectors | body text <12 px flagged (14 recommended, 16 ideal); functional UI text <11 px flagged (legal smallprint 10 px) [PRAC] |

Synthesis: 16 px for reading text on the web; 14 px acceptable for dense secondary UI text; never below 11–12 px for anything functional [HEUR].

**Line length (measure).** Butterick 45–90 characters including spaces (https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html) [PRAC]; Baymard 50–75 characters, ceiling 80, based on e-commerce testing observations and Ruder (Edward Scott, 2022-05-10, https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability) [EMP-qualitative]; WCAG 1.4.8 (AAA) ≤80 characters, **≤40 for CJK** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html) [STD]; Anthropic v3 below 80 characters; impeccable flags >80ch and recommends `max-width` 65–75ch. A widely repeated "skipped 41% more often" statistic attributed to Baymard does not appear in the Baymard article — **[unverified]**. Synthesis: target 45–75ch (`max-width: 65ch` is a good default), hard ceiling 80ch / 40 CJK characters.

**Line height (leading) by size.** Leading ratio should fall as size rises and rise as measure widens.

| Role | Material 3 | Apple iOS (Large default) | Others |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | 57/64 = 1.12 | Large Title 34/41 = 1.21 | Hallmark display 1.0–1.1, all-caps display ≥1.0 (1.02–1.08 recommended) |
| Headline/title | 32/40 = 1.25; 22/28 = 1.27 | Title 1 28/34 = 1.21 | gstack headings 1.15–1.25 |
| Body | 16/24 = 1.5; 14/20 = 1.43 | Body 17/22 = 1.29 | Butterick 1.20–1.45; WCAG 1.4.8 (AAA) ≥1.5; impeccable flags <1.3 for multi-line text, recommends 1.5–1.7 |
| Small | 12/16 = 1.33; 11/16 = 1.45 | Caption 2 11/13 = 1.18 | — |

Apple adds that 3+ lines should not use tight leading even where height is constrained (HIG). Also keep within WCAG 1.4.12 Text Spacing [STD]: content must not break when users set line-height 1.5, paragraph spacing 2×, letter-spacing 0.12em, word-spacing 0.16em (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html). Synthesis: long-form body 1.5 (1.4–1.6), dense UI body 1.3–1.45, headings 1.1–1.25, display 1.0–1.1; light-on-dark text gets slightly more leading, a touch more tracking and one weight step more (impeccable `typeset.md`) [HEUR/PRAC].

**Type scale.** A modular scale is a set of sizes related by a ratio, anchored on the body size or another important number (Tim Brown, "More Meaningful Typography", A List Apart, 2011-05-03, https://alistapart.com/article/more-meaningful-typography/) [PRAC]. Calibrations: Material uses Major Second (1.125) from a 14 base and warns against small differences between sizes [PLAT]; Utopia's fluid calculator defaults to 1.2 at 360 px → 1.25 at 1240 px with an 18→20 px base, emitted as `clamp()` (https://utopia.fyi/type/calculator/) [PRAC]; gstack suggests 1.25 or 1.333; impeccable flags any page whose largest step between adjacent role sizes is <1.25. Synthesis: product UI 1.125–1.2 with weight/colour doing extra work; marketing/editorial 1.25–1.333 with a larger jump for display; 4–6 sizes are usually enough (Material: most products need far fewer than its 15 styles). The Anthropic blog's advice of 3×+ size jumps and 100–200 vs 800–900 weight contrasts is an anti-blandness prompt for marketing heroes, not a general rule.

**Tracking (letter-spacing).**
- All caps and small caps: +5–12% (≈ `0.05em`–`0.12em`); do not letterspace lowercase except very small text (<9 pt); remove tracking from large lowercase headlines; if the gaps could fit another letter, it is too much (Butterick, https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html) [PRAC].
- Large display: slight negative tracking is normal; −0.04em is the floor (impeccable/gstack), −0.01 to −0.03em typical [PRAC].
- Font-specific: Apple's SF tracking table moves from +41/1000 em at 6 pt to 0 at 12 pt and −26/1000 em at 17 pt, then turns positive (+12…+14) above ~24–28 pt where the Display optical size takes over (HIG "Tracking values") — tracking must be tuned per face, not copied [PLAT]. Material's small labels carry ≈+0.04em (11–12 px with +0.5 px) [PLAT].
- Detector: impeccable `wide-tracking` flags >0.05em on body text; gstack's checklist forbids letterspacing on lowercase text.

**Weights and emphasis.** Use ≥2 weights for hierarchy (gstack); emphasised variants are medium/semibold/bold (Apple text styles specify an emphasised weight per style; Material 3 Expressive adds 15 "emphasized" styles) [PLAT]. Minimise bold/italic and never combine them; avoid underlining except links; all caps only for less than one line (Butterick summary) [PRAC]. Emphasise by de-emphasising competitors rather than escalating everything (Refactoring UI, see §3.12).

**Numerals and typographic details.**
- Tabular figures for columns and comparisons, proportional in running text; lining figures are always safe, oldstyle suit lowercase body text but never with caps (Butterick, https://practicaltypography.com/alternate-figures.html) [PRAC]; CSS `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (Vercel MUST) [PRAC].
- Curly quotes, the `…` character, `text-wrap: balance` on headings and `pretty` on paragraphs, non-breaking spaces in `10 MB`/`⌘ K`, locale-aware `Intl` formatting (Vercel guidelines; ui-skills `baseline-ui`) [PRAC].
- Proper dashes are part of good typography (Butterick lists correct dashes among required characters); flag em-dash *density* in copy, never the character itself (§5 C5).
- CJK note: WCAG's 40-character measure applies; negative tracking and Latin-centric caps rules do not transfer — CJK layout needs its own research (W3C *Requirements for Chinese Text Layout*, https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/) **[gap]**.

### 3.2 Colour

**Work in a perceptual space.** Oklab/OKLCH (Björn Ottosson, 2020-12-23, https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) was designed to predict lightness, chroma and hue well, keep them orthogonal, and blend smoothly; CIELAB mispredicts blue hue (blue drifts to purple as lightness changes) and HSV/HSL lightness is uneven across hues [PRAC/technical]. Evil Martians (Sitnik & Turner, updated 2025-09-17, https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl): equal OKLCH L gives predictable contrast across hues; generate shades by changing L (`oklch(from var(--c) calc(l - 0.1) c h)`); OKLCH can express P3; caveats — not every L/C/H is displayable, max chroma differs per hue, browsers clip to the nearest displayable colour so manual gamut mapping is better [PRAC]. impeccable adds: when deriving ramps, reduce chroma near white and black instead of holding it constant (`colorize.md`).

**Build roles, not swatches.** Define canvas/elevated surfaces, primary/secondary text, action/focus/selection, borders/separators, success/warning/error/info, and data scales (impeccable `colorize.md`). A good concrete model is Radix Colors' 12-step scale: 1–2 app/subtle backgrounds, 3–5 component backgrounds (rest/hover/active), 6–8 borders (subtle/UI + focus/hover), 9–10 solid fills, 11–12 low/high-contrast text; steps 11 and 12 are guaranteed APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90 on step 2 of the same scale (https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale) [PLAT-like]. Tailwind's 50–950 ramps and Material tonal palettes serve the same purpose.

**Tinted neutrals.** Material's dark theme shows the mechanism: overlaying 8% of the primary on `#121212` yields a branded dark surface `#1F1B24` (https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html) [PLAT]. Vercel: tint borders/shadows/text toward the background hue on non-neutral backgrounds [PRAC]. Hallmark requires ≥0.005 OKLCH chroma toward the anchor hue for every neutral (except a monochrome "modern-minimal" genre) whereas impeccable says neutral grey is valid when it serves the world — resolved in §5 C7: tinting is a choice, but one temperature must be used consistently (gstack: warm *or* cool neutrals, not mixed; taste-skill: one grey family per project).

**Colour strategy and accent restraint.** Pick a strategy before picking colours (impeccable `new-work.md`, gstack): *Restrained* (neutrals + one accent; default for Operate/Read), *Committed* (one saturated colour carries 30–60% of the surface), *Full palette* (3–4 named roles), *Drenched* (the surface is the colour). Restraint calibrations: OpenAI — one accent by default; ui-skills — one accent per view; Hallmark — accent ≤~5% of any viewport's area (atmospheric genres ≤20%) [PRAC]. Anthropic's guidance favours a dominant colour with sharp accents over timid, evenly distributed palettes [PRAC]. Saturated accents should own a role (action, selection, state), not be sprinkled.

**Semantic colour.** Keep meanings stable and respect domain conventions rather than assuming hues (impeccable); never encode by colour alone — WCAG 1.4.1 [STD]; give charts colour-blind-safe palettes and redundant encodings (Vercel) [PRAC]; AIM's colour-blindness simulation metric (Machado et al. 2009) can check this (§4.4).

**Dark mode.** Compose it, don't invert (impeccable). Material: use dark grey `#121212` rather than black so elevation and shadows remain visible; higher elevation = lighter surface via white overlays (0dp 0%, 1dp 5%, 2dp 7%, 3dp 8%, 4dp 9%, 6dp 11%, 8dp 12%, 12dp 14%, 16dp 15%, 24dp 16%); white body text on dark surfaces ≥15.8:1; text opacities 87% (high emphasis), 60% (medium/hint), 38% (disabled); prefer lighter, desaturated tones (200–50) because saturated colours fail 4.5:1 and "vibrate" on dark grounds (https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html) [PLAT]. Set `color-scheme: dark` and a matching `theme-color` (Vercel) [PRAC]. gstack heuristics: off-white text (~`#E0E0E0`) rather than pure white; desaturate the accent 10–20% in dark mode [PRAC]. Choose light or dark from the use scene (who, where, ambient light), not the category (impeccable, gstack).

**Contrast.** WCAG 2.x is the normative baseline: 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text (≥24 px regular or ≥18.66 px bold), 3:1 for UI components, icons and focus indicators (SC 1.4.3, 1.4.11) [STD]. APCA (WCAG 3 draft work, not yet normative) "Bronze simple mode": body text Lc 75 minimum / Lc 90 preferred, other content text Lc 60, large (>36 px) Lc 45, and large text should not exceed Lc 90 (https://readtech.org/ARC/tests/bronze-simple-mode/?tn=criterion) [proposed STD]. Vercel recommends APCA; Hallmark accepts WCAG 4.5:1 or APCA Lc ≥60 for body. Use WCAG for pass/fail, APCA as a quality signal (§5 C8). Interactions should *increase* contrast on hover/active/focus (Vercel). Gray text on coloured surfaces should be tinted from the surface hue, never neutral grey (impeccable; Refactoring UI).

### 3.3 Spacing, grids, layout and density

- **Base unit.** 8-point grid for dimensions/padding/margins with a 4-point baseline for type ("most popular screen sizes are divisible by 8"; spec.fm "The 8-Point Grid", https://spec.fm/specifics/8-pt-grid) [PRAC]. impeccable: a 4-unit base supplies the middle steps an 8-only scale misses; Hallmark: all spacing on a named scale in multiples of 4 px. IBM Carbon tokens: 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80, 96, 160 px (https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/spacing/overview/) [PLAT] — a good default ramp.
- **Proximity and rhythm.** Group by meaning before adding containers; tight intervals within groups, generous between groups; more space above a heading than below it so it binds to its content (impeccable `heading-rhythm` detector; gstack: a heading must sit visibly closer to the section it introduces) [PRAC]. Monotonous spacing (one value >60% of uses) is a tell (§2.4.3).
- **Whitespace.** Start with too much and remove; you don't have to fill the screen (Refactoring UI) [PRAC]. Hallmark caps hero vertical bloat: bottom padding ≥1.3× top, hero essentials visible at 1280×800 [PRAC].
- **Optical alignment.** Align deliberately to grid/baseline/edges; nudge ±1 px when perception beats geometry (icons in buttons, play triangles, round shapes overshooting) (Vercel guidelines) [PRAC]. Make optical corrections only after inspecting the render (impeccable `layout.md`).
- **Density.** Match density to use frequency and task (impeccable). Material density scale: 0, −1, −2, −3, each step −4 px of component height; increase horizontal spacing as vertical spacing decreases; touch targets stay ≥48 px (https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/grids-spacing/density; https://m2.material.io/develop/web/supporting/density) [PLAT]. Targets: WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) ≥24×24 CSS px [STD]; Apple 44×44 pt; Material 48×48 dp [PLAT]; Vercel: expand hit area when visual <24 px, ≥44 px on mobile.
- **Responsive.** Verify at 320/375/414/768 and desktop widths; no horizontal scroll from 320 to 1920 px; `minmax(0,1fr)` for image tracks; `overflow-wrap:anywhere; min-width:0` on display text (Hallmark gates 34, 49–51) [PRAC]. Check ultra-wide by zooming out to 50% (Vercel).

### 3.4 Hierarchy

- Not everything is equally important: decide primary/secondary/tertiary and express it with *size, weight, colour/contrast and position*, preferring weight and colour over size inside product UI (Refactoring UI) [PRAC].
- Two-second test: can a reader name primary, secondary and tertiary content in two seconds (Hallmark axis B)? Squint/blur test: does the order survive blurring (impeccable `layout.md`; gstack) [PRAC].
- First-three-fixations check: name the first three things the eye goes to and compare with intent (gstack design-review Phase 1) [PRAC]; saliency models (UMSI, MD-EAM in AIM, §4.4) can approximate this computationally [EMP].
- One primary action per view; labels are a last resort — let the data and its formatting speak (Refactoring UI); separate visual hierarchy from document (heading-level) hierarchy [PRAC].
- Scan-by-headlines litmus: the page should be understandable from headlines alone (OpenAI) [PRAC].

### 3.5 Depth: shadows, borders, elevation

- One consistent light source: all shadows share an offset ratio (typically vertical ≈ 2× horizontal); higher elevation → larger offset and blur, lower opacity (Josh W. Comeau, "Designing Beautiful Shadows in CSS", 2021-09-13, updated 2026-04-27, https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/) [PRAC].
- Layer shadows: several stacked shadows with doubling offset/blur produce smoother, more realistic falloff (Tobias Ahlin, 2019-09-19, https://tobiasahlin.com/blog/layered-smooth-box-shadows/: `0 1px 1px`, `0 2px 2px`, `0 4px 4px`, `0 8px 8px`, `0 16px 16px` at low alpha); Vercel: ≥2 layers (ambient + direct) [PRAC].
- Colour-match shadows to the background hue instead of transparent black (Comeau; taste-skill also tints shadows to the background hue) [PRAC].
- Declare elevation once: border *or* shadow, not a hairline plus a wide soft shadow (impeccable `<codex>` rule; §2.4.4 A-SU3); semi-transparent borders can sharpen edges when combined deliberately (Vercel "crisp borders") — the difference is intent and a single elevation system [PRAC].
- In dark mode, express elevation with lighter surfaces/overlays because shadows are hard to see (Material) [PLAT]. Use fewer borders; separate with spacing or background shifts (Refactoring UI). Depth must have an offset; zero-offset coloured halos are decoration (impeccable/gstack).
- Litmus: would the design still feel premium with all decorative shadows removed? (OpenAI) [PRAC].

### 3.6 Corner radius

- Choose a small radius *scale* tied to component size and a documented rule (e.g. buttons pill, cards 12–16 px, inputs 8 px) and apply it everywhere (taste-skill "shape consistency lock"; impeccable `<codex>` card radii 12–16 px, pills only for small controls) [PRAC].
- Nested radii must be concentric: child ≤ parent; inner radius = outer radius − gap/padding (Vercel; gstack design-review checklist) [PRAC].
- Flag uniformity: >80% of radii identical and ≥16 px (gstack); a rounded card with a thick one-side border clashes (impeccable `border-accent-on-rounded`).

### 3.7 Icon systems

- One family, one stroke weight, one grid; never mix libraries or substitute emoji/unicode glyphs (Hallmark gate 30; impeccable craft floor) [PRAC].
- Specs of the dominant open set: Lucide draws on a 24×24 canvas with a ≥1 px safe zone, 2 px strokes, round caps and joins (`lucide-icons/lucide docs/contribute/icons/design-principles.md`) [PLAT-like]. Material Symbols expose FILL 0–1, weight 100–700, grade −50…200 and optical size 20–48 dp; match icon weight/grade to adjacent text, use a low grade (e.g. −25) for light icons on dark backgrounds, let optical size adjust stroke as icons scale (https://developers.google.com/fonts/docs/material_symbols) [PLAT]. SF Symbols come in weights matching SF text weights so symbols and text align (HIG) [PLAT].
- Choose icons for meaning; label ambiguous ones. The ✨ sparkle was not associated with AI by any of 107 participants (NN/g, Kaplan 2024) [EMP]. Icon-only buttons need an accessible name (Vercel) [STD-adjacent].

### 3.8 Imagery and illustration direction

- Users attend to information-carrying images (real people, products, task-relevant images) and ignore decorative "feel-good" stock; e.g. real team photos got 10% more viewing time than much larger biography text in one eyetracking case (Jakob Nielsen, 2010-10-31, reviewed 2026, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/) [EMP].
- Imagery should show the product, place, atmosphere or context; prefer in-situ photography over generic 3D objects; keep strong contrast for text over images; avoid images with embedded logos or typographic clutter (OpenAI GPT-5.4 guide) [PRAC]. Text over images needs consistent contrast: scrim/overlay, reduce image contrast, colourise, or a subtle text shadow (Refactoring UI) [PRAC].
- Real art direction or none: no shape-assembled SVG scenes, sketchy doodles or fake product chrome (§2.4.5). If assets are missing, leave labelled slots and tell the user (taste-skill §4.8; Hallmark).
- Generated imagery carries its own tells (yellow cast, waxy skin, the "same face", missing sensor noise) — signs-of-ai-design Part II; treat as weak signals in combination.
- One illustration style, palette and line weight across the product [PRAC/HEUR].

### 3.9 Motion

**Whether to animate (frequency).** Actions performed 100+ times a day (keyboard shortcuts, command palette) get no animation; tens of times a day → remove or drastically reduce; occasional (modals, drawers, toasts) → standard; rare/first-run → room for delight; never animate keyboard-initiated actions (Emil Kowalski, `emilkowalski/skills` `review-animations/STANDARDS.md`, MIT) [PRAC]. Rauno Freiberg reached the same conclusion from building a keyboard-driven tool: animations that felt good at first felt sluggish after days of use, and macOS context menus and the app switcher appear without motion ("Invisible Details of Interaction Design", July 2023, https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design) [PRAC].

**Duration.**

| Source | Values |
|---|---|
| NN/g (Page Laubheimer, 2020-02-09, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/) [EMP-informed PRAC] | simple feedback ≈100 ms; larger changes (modals) 200–300 ms; most UI 100–400 ms (400 is very slow); exits shorter than entrances (200–250 vs 300); longer for larger distance; shorter for frequent animations |
| Material 3 tokens (`_md-sys-motion.scss`) [PLAT] | short 50/100/150/200 ms; medium 250/300/350/400; long 450–600; extra-long 700–1000 |
| Emil Kowalski [PRAC] | button press 100–160 ms; tooltips/small popovers 125–200; dropdowns 150–250; modals/drawers 200–500; UI animations stay under 300 ms; stagger 30–80 ms per item |
| impeccable `animate.md` [PRAC] | 100–150 feedback; 150–300 state change; 300–500 layout/overlay/view; 500–800 one authored focal entrance |
| ui-skills `baseline-ui` [PRAC] | ≤200 ms for interaction feedback |
| Vercel [PRAC] | spinners: show-delay 150–300 ms, minimum visible 300–500 ms to avoid flicker |

**Easing.** Entering elements ease-out, exiting ease-in, avoid linear for movement (NN/g). Emil Kowalski argues for ease-out on both enter and exit because ease-in delays the moment the user is watching, with strong custom curves (`cubic-bezier(0.23,1,0.32,1)` ease-out; `cubic-bezier(0.77,0,0.175,1)` ease-in-out for on-screen movement; iOS-like drawer `cubic-bezier(0.32,0.72,0,1)`), `ease` for hover/colour, linear only for constant motion. Material 3: standard `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)`, emphasized-decelerate `(0.05,0.7,0.1,1)`, emphasized-accelerate `(0.3,0,0.8,0.15)`; M3 Expressive springs use damping 0.9 for spatial motion (slight overshoot) and 1.0 for effects (no overshoot). impeccable recommends exponential ease-out such as `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` and rejects bounce/elastic by reflex; Emil keeps spring bounce 0.1–0.3 for drag and playful interactions. Resolution in §5 C9.

**Physicality and polish.** Never animate from `scale(0)` — start at 0.9–0.97 with opacity 0; popovers scale from their trigger (`transform-origin`), modals from centre; pressed buttons `scale(0.97)` with ~160 ms ease-out (Kowalski) [PRAC]. Prefer interruptible CSS transitions over keyframes for UI that toggles rapidly; `@starting-style` for entry (Kowalski). Gestures: dismiss on velocity (≈0.11 px/ms) not only distance; damping/friction at boundaries; pointer capture; ignore extra touches (Kowalski; Rauno on momentum and responsive gestures) [PRAC].

**What not to animate.** Layout properties (width/height/top/left/margin/padding) and `transition: all` (Vercel MUST NOT); large blur/backdrop-filter surfaces; anything keyboard-initiated; identical entrances on every section; content hidden until a reveal fires (impeccable `content-hidden-at-rest`); hover-zoom on images (§2.4.6). Animate only `transform`/`opacity` for routine UI (Vercel, Kowalski, ui-skills); Framer Motion's `x/y/scale` shorthands run on the main thread — use full transform strings (Kowalski) [PRAC].

**Accessibility.** Every animation needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` path that removes spatial movement but keeps meaningful opacity/colour feedback (impeccable, Kowalski, Vercel); auto-playing motion >5 s alongside content needs pause/stop/hide (WCAG 2.2.2) [STD]; gate hover motion with `(hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` (Kowalski).

**Process.** One authored moment beats scattered effects (Anthropic v2/v3; impeccable; gstack); review motion in slow motion (2–5× duration) and on real devices, and again the next day (Kowalski) [PRAC].

### 3.10 Interaction polish details (the "invisible" layer)

From Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines (MIT) unless noted — each is checkable: visible `:focus-visible` rings never covered by sticky elements (and WCAG 2.4.11 focus-not-obscured [STD]); hit targets ≥24 px (44 px mobile); never block paste; loading buttons keep their label and show a spinner; URL reflects state (filters, tabs, pagination); Back/Forward restores scroll; destructive actions confirm or offer Undo; optimistic UI with rollback; tooltips delay the first and show peers instantly; `overscroll-behavior: contain` in modals/drawers; inline errors next to fields with focus moved to the first error on submit; skeletons mirror final layout; handle empty, sparse, dense and error states; long content truncates (`min-w-0` in flex children); locale-aware numbers and dates. Hallmark adds: focus rings appear instantly (no fade); tooltip hover delay 800–1000 ms vs 0 ms on focus; no celebratory toast for effects the user can already see; inputs keep `border-width` constant across states and match adjacent button height (44 px floor); disabled = opacity + `cursor:not-allowed` + `disabled` attribute; reserve helper-text height so errors don't shift layout. impeccable/gstack add: theme browser surfaces (`::selection`, `caret-color`, `accent-color`, scrollbars, `text-underline-offset`, tabular numerals). Rauno: trigger lightweight actions during a gesture but destructive ones only on gesture end; responsive gestures apply deltas immediately before a threshold animation; motion should respect spatial origin.

### 3.11 Microcopy voice

- Words exist to make the interface easier to understand and use; name things from the user's side ("notifications", not "webhook config"); describe rather than sell; specific beats clever (Anthropic v2/v3 writing section) [PRAC].
- Active voice; controls state their outcome ("Save changes", not "Submit"); one action keeps one name through the flow ("Publish" → "Published"); errors explain what happened and how to fix it without apologising or blaming; empty states invite the next action; sentence case, plain verbs, no filler (Anthropic) [PRAC]. Vercel's house style (Title Case headings/buttons, `&` over "and", numerals for counts) is explicitly brand-specific — pick conventions per product and apply them consistently.
- Error messages: visible near the problem, not colour-only, plain language, precise, constructive, no blame, preserve user input, avoid premature errors (Tim Neusesser & Evan Sunwall, NN/g, 2023-05-14, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/) [PRAC/EMP-informed].
- Tone has four measurable dimensions — formal↔casual, serious↔funny, respectful↔irreverent, matter-of-fact↔enthusiastic; tone shifts produced statistically significant (p<.05) differences in perceived friendliness/formality etc. among 50 respondents (Kate Moran, NN/g, 2016-07-17, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/) [EMP]. Write the target position on each dimension into the design brief.
- Cut happy talk and instructions; omit needless words (gstack, after Krug); "if deleting 30% of the copy improves it, keep deleting" (OpenAI) [PRAC].

### 3.12 Refactoring UI, summarised in our own words

*Refactoring UI* (Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger; copyrighted — principles paraphrased; chapter list at https://www.refactoringui.com/book/table-of-contents): **Starting from scratch** — design a real feature before the shell; work in low fidelity and postpone detail; pick a personality (via font, colour, radius, language) and limit choices with predefined systems for type, spacing, colour, shadows. **Hierarchy** — not all elements are equal; use weight and colour before size; de-emphasise secondary content instead of shouting primary content; avoid grey text on coloured backgrounds; labels are a last resort; visual hierarchy is separate from document semantics; balance weight against contrast (heavy icons need softer colour). **Layout and spacing** — start with too much white space; use a constrained spacing/sizing scale with meaningful jumps; don't fill the whole screen; grids are tools, not laws; relative sizing does not scale linearly; avoid ambiguous spacing (more space between groups than within). **Text** — a hand-crafted type scale, good fonts, a comfortable line length, baseline alignment for mixed sizes, line-height proportional to size and measure, not every link needs colour, align for readability, tighten tracking on large headlines and loosen on all-caps. **Colour** — use a perceptual-friendly model (the book uses HSL; OKLCH now does this better, §3.2), you need more colours than you think (8–10 shades for primaries, greys and semantic colours), define shades up front, keep saturation up as lightness moves away from 50%, greys can be tinted warm or cool, accessible doesn't have to mean ugly, never rely on colour alone. **Depth** — emulate a light source, use shadows to convey elevation, two-part shadows (a tight dark one and a soft large one), flat designs can still have depth through colour and overlap. **Images** — use good photos, guarantee text contrast over images, respect each asset's intended size (don't scale icons up), plan for user-uploaded content. **Finishing touches** — upgrade defaults (bullets, quotes, inputs), add colour with accent borders, decorate backgrounds subtly, design empty states, use fewer borders, think beyond the obvious component.

Observation **[inference]**: the book's "add colour with accent borders" and the Tailwind UI kits by the same authors are a plausible upstream source of the coloured side-stripe card that is now the most-cited AI tell (A-SU1) — a craft tip that became a default once replicated at scale. The lesson for UI-Evaluator is the same as §2.1: a good move used without a reason becomes a tell.

## 4. Part C — Making taste operational for an agent

"Taste" cannot be shipped as a list of good fonts (§2.1). What can be shipped is (a) a **decision procedure** that forces brief-specific choices, (b) **references** that inject outside taste legally, (c) **rubrics** that make judgement observable, and (d) **measurements** that catch defects and convergence. Practitioners frame taste as trained rather than innate — built by studying why great interfaces feel right (Emil Kowalski, `emil-design-eng/SKILL.md`) — which is exactly what a procedure + references + critique loop simulates.

### 4.1 From brand attributes to an intentional aesthetic direction

**Inputs to pin before any pixels** (each source independently converges on these):
1. *Product truth and subject* — what it is, for whom, the page's single job; distinctive choices come from the subject's own world, materials and vernacular (Anthropic v2/v3 subject grounding; impeccable `PRODUCT.md`; taste-skill one-line "design read").
2. *Surface mode* — Persuade / Operate / Read / Experience; choose per surface, not per product (impeccable; gstack classifier). Mode sets how much expression is allowed.
3. *Use scene* — who, where, under what light; this decides light vs dark (impeccable, gstack).
4. *Brand attributes* — 3–5 adjectives with explicit "this, not that" pairs (e.g. *precise, not cold*). Aaker's brand-personality model (42 traits in five dimensions: sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness; 631 respondents rating 37 brands; *Journal of Marketing Research* 34, 1997, pp. 347–356) is a validated vocabulary for this step [EMP]. For copy, place the voice on NN/g's four tone dimensions (§3.11) [EMP].
5. *Constraints and real content* — existing brand assets, accessibility needs, performance budget, real copy and data (Hallmark pre-flight scan; impeccable; Anthropic asks for the brief's real content throughout).

**Attribute → visual lever mapping** [HEUR; assembled from Refactoring UI's idea that personality is carried by font, colour, radius and language, Material's brand-vs-plain type roles, Kowalski's point that motion should match a component's personality, and NN/g tone]:

| Lever | Questions the attribute must answer | Example contrasts |
|---|---|---|
| Typeface structure | serif/sans/slab/mono; geometric vs humanist vs grotesque; width; contrast; optical size | *precise* → tight grotesque, tabular figures; *warm* → humanist forms, generous x-height; *authoritative* → high-contrast serif used sparingly |
| Scale & weight | ratio, number of steps, weight spread | *calm* → 1.125–1.2, 2 weights; *bold* → 1.333+, one heavy display |
| Colour strategy | Restrained / Committed / Full / Drenched; temperature; chroma | *trustworthy operational tool* → Restrained; *energetic consumer launch* → Committed or Drenched |
| Shape | radius scale, corner style, line weight | *engineered* → small radii, hairlines; *friendly* → larger radii, pills only for small controls |
| Density & space | spacing ramp position, density level | *expert tool* → Material density −1/−2; *editorial* → generous measure and leading |
| Depth | flat / borders / layered shadows / surfaces | *serious* → borders and surface shifts; *tactile* → layered, hue-tinted shadows |
| Imagery | photography / illustration / data / product UI; art direction | *credible* → real product, real people; *imaginative* → commissioned illustration |
| Motion | frequency budget, duration band, easing character | *fast tool* → near-zero motion on frequent actions; *playful* → springs with small bounce on rare moments |
| Copy | tone dimensions, vocabulary, sentence case | *matter-of-fact* vs *enthusiastic*; product nouns from the user's side |

**Diverge, then converge** (procedures from the sources):
- Generate several *materially different* directions from the audience's cultural world — impeccable asks for seven candidate visual systems, artifacts, places or rituals that the audience knows intimately, spanning ≥3 material families, explicitly excluding the page the category always ships and its obvious opposite; on Operate/Read surfaces the candidates must not be costumes of the tools the audience operates (a terminal for developers) (`new-work.md`) [PRAC].
- Break ranking ruts with randomness: impeccable's `concept-seed` script deals three of the candidates so runs do not always pick the same top-ranked idea [PRAC]. Railcode's founder generated 4–10 variants per concept along a minimal→outlandish spectrum, half guided and half "go wild", then forked and compared side by side, tuning colour/type with throwaway in-page pickers (§2.2) [PRAC].
- Self-check for convergence before building: write the plan, then mentally run a similar prompt — if a similar brief would land in the same place, revise that part and say why (Anthropic v2/v3); ask whether the look could be guessed from the category alone, or from the category plus avoidance (impeccable/gstack); do not reuse the face shipped in the last three projects (avoid-ai-design); keep a project log so successive outputs differ structurally, not just in colour (Hallmark `.hallmark/log.json`) [PRAC].
- **Typicality vs novelty — the evidence.** Low visual complexity and *high prototypicality* produced the best first-impression aesthetics ratings, with effects present at 17–50 ms exposure (Tuch et al., *IJHCS* 70(11), 2012, 119 website screenshots) [EMP]; people prefer novelty that does not destroy typicality — "most advanced, yet acceptable" (Hekkert, Snelders & van Wieringen, *Br. J. Psychology* 94, 2003, DOI 10.1348/000712603762842147) [EMP]; typical designs win at low exposure while atypical designs gain liking with repeated exposure (Landwehr, Wentzel & Herrmann, *J. Marketing* 77, 2013 — cars, not UIs) [EMP]. Implication: keep *structural* conventions (navigation placement, control types, form patterns, page anatomy users expect) and spend distinctiveness on the *brand layer* (type, colour, imagery, voice, one signature moment) and on real content — which is also how impeccable treats Operate/Read surfaces (borrow type, density, palette and one signature move; keep standard navigation and controls). Anti-slop must not become anti-convention.
- **Record the decision as a contract** before code: Anthropic's plan (palette as 4–6 named hex values, type roles, layout concept with ASCII wireframes, alignment, principles); impeccable's six-block direction contract (THESIS, OWN-WORLD, STORY, FIRST VIEWPORT, FORM, FINISH); persisted as tokens in `DESIGN.md` — Google Labs' open DESIGN.md format (https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md, Apache-2.0, repo created 2026-04-10): YAML front-matter tokens (colors, typography, rounded, spacing, components) plus markdown rationale; `npx @google/design.md lint DESIGN.md` checks broken token references and WCAG contrast, `npx @google/design.md diff` reports token-level regressions — so later pages cannot drift (A-FP2). Note the propagation risk again: the spec README's own example is a warm "limestone" neutral, a terracotta "Boston Clay" accent, broadsheet framing and Space Grotesk caps labels — i.e. the second-order default of §2.3. Examples in specs and skills become defaults; ours must be deliberately varied or abstract.

Ethics rules distilled: study the *DNA* (macrostructure, type-pairing logic, colour anchor, density, signature interaction) rather than copying assets — Hallmark's `study` verb "never copies pixels", refuses template-marketplace URLs, and requires attestation before turning a URL's DNA into a portable `design.md`; never lift logos, illustrations, photography, copy or code; verify font and icon licences (§3.1); blend several references so no single site is reproduced; keep the reference list in the decision record. (Not legal advice: UI "look and feel" is protected unevenly across jurisdictions, but assets, text and code clearly are.)

### 4.3 Rubrics for distinctiveness and polish

Existing rubrics, compared:

| Rubric | Items | Scoring | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI litmus (GPT-5.4 guide) | 7 yes/no: brand unmistakable in first screen; one strong visual anchor; understandable from headlines only; one job per section; cards necessary; motion improves hierarchy; premium without decorative shadows. Plus 7 hard rejections | binary | Landing-page oriented |
| Hallmark pre-emit critique | Philosophy, Hierarchy, Execution, Specificity, Restraint, Variety | 1–5 each; any <3 → revise; stamped in the file | Variety scored by *structural* distance from previous outputs |

| gstack design-review | 10 categories (~80 items) incl. AI-slop; first impression; trunk test | A–F per category: each high-impact finding −1 grade, medium −½; weights (hierarchy/type/spacing 15% each … slop 5%) + separate AI-Slop grade | Mixes craft and usability |
| Krebs slop cop | 14–16 deterministic patterns | count tiers: 0–1 low, 2–3 medium, 4+ high | Labeled data (192) and P/R script available |

Observations: (1) the rubrics agree on *specificity*, *hierarchy*, *restraint* and *execution* as the core axes; (2) none is validated against human judgements; (3) human taste raters disagree substantially — in UIClip's BetterApp data 12 designers reached only Krippendorff's α = 0.37 on 10 shared pairs — so rubric items must be anchored to observable evidence, and comparisons should be **pairwise** (UI-Bench: 300 sites from 30 prompts, 4,000+ expert pairwise judgements, TrueSkill; Design Arena: crowd pairwise votes aggregated with Bradley–Terry; https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20410, https://notes.designarena.ai/methodology/) rather than absolute.

Proposed UI-Evaluator rubric [HEUR, to be validated]: **Specificity** (swap test + category-guess test + similar-prompt test; 0–4 with written evidence), **Hierarchy** (2-second primary/secondary/tertiary naming; squint test; first-three-fixations vs intent), **Craft floor** (pass/fail on measurable checks in §3: contrast, measure, leading, target size, states, reduced motion, focus visibility, no overflow 320–1920 px), **Restraint** (one boldness; decoration with a job; tell density), **Coherence** (tokens honoured; one radius/elevation/icon system; cross-page consistency), **Honesty** (no invented claims, people, logos or chrome — pass/fail), plus a **Tell report** (§2.4 IDs, severity P0–P2, reliability). Score Specificity *before* showing detector output to the judge (impeccable's isolation rule).

How to run (from the README): Docker — `cp .env.example .env && docker-compose up --build`, then open `http://localhost:8080`; or native — Python 3.7 (conda env), MongoDB, Chrome + matching ChromeDriver (`scripts/get_webdrivers.sh`), Node 16 for the frontend; batch mode without the web app: `python screenshoter.py -i urls.csv -sw 1280 -sh 800 -f -o out/` then `python evaluator.py -i <screenshot_dir> -m m1,m2,m3 -p -o <results_dir>` (writes `results.csv`, `quantiles.csv`, per-metric JSON and optional histograms). Caveats: dated toolchain (Python 3.7, Node 16), several metrics are CPU-heavy, and most metrics are *descriptive* (how complex/colourful/aligned), not verdicts — interpret against category baselines and against the design's intended mode.

**Validity evidence for these metric families**:
- Miniukovich & De Angeli, "Computation of Interface Aesthetics" (CHI '15, DOI 10.1145/2702123.2702575): eight metrics (visual clutter, colour range, number of dominant colours, figure-ground contrast, contour congestion, symmetry, grid quality, white space) explained up to **49%** of variance in webpage aesthetics (N=62) and up to **32%** for iPhone apps (N=53, with genre), at 150 ms and 4 s exposures [EMP].
- Reinecke et al., CHI '13 (DOI 10.1145/2470654.2481281): perceived colourfulness and complexity models, plus demographics, explained ~half the variance of appeal ratings after 500 ms (548 volunteers, 450 websites) [EMP].
- First impressions are fast and sticky: appeal ratings at 50 ms correlate highly with 500 ms (Lindgaard et al., *Behaviour & IT* 25(2), 2006, DOI 10.1080/01449290500330448) [EMP]; complexity and prototypicality act within 17–50 ms (Tuch et al. 2012) [EMP].
- Classic layout-aesthetic formulas (balance, equilibrium, symmetry, sequence, cohesion, unity, proportion, simplicity, density, regularity, economy, homogeneity, rhythm — Ngo, Teo & Byrne, *Information Sciences* 152, 2003) remain a source of computable layout features; their validation used small, dated stimulus sets **[not re-validated here]**.
- AI-generated UIs specifically: a 2026 within-subject study (62 participants; 12 interfaces = 4 scenarios × human-designed / raw AI / prompt-optimised AI) found raw AI UIs had the highest perceived cognitive load and complexity and only 63.1% task accuracy vs 100% for human-designed and prompt-optimised versions; prompt-optimised AI UIs improved but stayed significantly different from human designs; correlations between pixel metrics (grayscale/colour entropy, Canny edge density, RMS contrast, white-space ratio) and user outcomes were only weak to moderate (*Electronics* 15(15):3458, 2026-08-05, https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/15/3458) [EMP; small stimulus set, static screenshots].
- Conclusion: pixel metrics are useful for *regression* and *outlier* detection (e.g. sudden clutter jumps, colour-count explosions, failed contrast), not for certifying beauty or distinctiveness.

**UIClip** — Jason Wu, Yi-Hao Peng, Amanda Li, Amanda Swearngin, Jeffrey P. Bigham, Jeffrey Nichols, UIST 2024 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12500; paper text CC BY 4.0). A CLIP ViT-B/32 dual encoder fine-tuned to score a screenshot against "ui screenshot. well-designed. <description>". Data: **JitterWeb**, 2.3M synthetic examples made by crawling websites and applying up to three "jitter" functions that inject defects inspired by the CRAP principles (contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity — colour swaps/noise, font size swaps, spacing and layout noise, complexity removal); **BetterApp**, ~1,200 ratings → 892 usable pairwise preferences on VINS app screens from 12 designers (Krippendorff's α = 0.37 on a shared subset). Results: best variant 75.12% average pairwise accuracy (87.1% on detecting jittered web defects); most baselines including GPT-4V near chance on choosing the better design; base CLIP even associated dark, unreadable backgrounds with better design. Applications: ranking generated code candidates, design-tip generation, quality-aware example retrieval. Release: MIT-licensed models on Hugging Face (`biglab/uiclip_jitteredwebsites-2-224-paraphrased_webpairs_humanpairs`, loadable with `transformers.CLIPModel`; 224×224 input). Limits for our purpose **[inference]**: trained to separate intact from *broken* designs, at 224 px, so it should catch gross CRAP violations and help rank variants, but it was never trained to separate *competent-but-generic* from *distinctive* — it cannot be our slop detector.

**LLM critique datasets**: UICrit (Duan, Chen, Li, Hartmann, Li; UIST 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850): 3,059 critiques with quality ratings for 983 mobile UIs from seven experienced designers; few-shot and visual prompting improved LLM-generated feedback by 55% [EMP]. Duan et al., CHI 2024 (GPT-4 heuristic feedback in a Figma plugin; 51 UIs, three heuristic sets): accurate and helpful for subtle errors and text, but usefulness decreased over iterations [EMP]. Implication: use critique exemplars (few-shot) and visual grounding; don't trust repeated self-critique rounds — consistent with impeccable's bounded verification passes.

**Measuring distinctiveness (convergence) directly** [HEUR, proposed]: evidence that generative assistance homogenises output exists for stories (Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances* 10(28), 2024: AI-assisted stories rated more creative individually but more similar to each other) and for images (Hintze 2025). For UI-Evaluator: (1) **cross-output similarity** — embed screenshots of N outputs for *different* briefs (CLIP/UIClip image embeddings or perceptual hashes) and report mean pairwise similarity; rising similarity = convergence; (2) **structural fingerprint** — section sequence and component-type sequence of the DOM (cf. Hallmark's macrostructure stamp), compared by edit distance/Jaccard across outputs and against the cookie-cutter template (A-LA3); (3) **token fingerprint** — primary families, accent hue bucket, radius set, shadow strings, compared with the versioned tell list (A-TY1/2, A-CO1–5) and Krebs' patterns; (4) a **labelled set** — reuse Krebs' 192 labels and extend with our own human labels so detectors report precision/recall instead of assumed accuracy.

### 4.5 An operational loop for an agent (synthesis of the sources; detailed in the ADOPT list)

1. **Pin the brief**: subject, audience, job, mode, use scene, 3–5 attributes as this-not-that pairs, tone positions, constraints, real content. Ask at most one or two questions; otherwise state the read (taste-skill, impeccable, Anthropic).
2. **Gather references** (≥3 outside the category, 1–2 inside), legally; extract DNA notes, not assets.
3. **Diverge**: ≥3 directions from the subject's world; exclude the category default and its predictable opposite and today's saturated looks (versioned list); pick by product clarity and audience identification, optionally with a random tie-break.
4. **Plan tokens**: OKLCH colour roles with a named strategy; type roles, scale, measure and leading; spacing and radius scales; one elevation system; motion tokens and a frequency budget; copy voice. Write them to `DESIGN.md`.
5. **Pre-build review**: similar-prompt test, category-guess test, swap test; revise what is generic and say why.
6. **Build with locked tokens**; real content or labelled placeholders; no invented claims.
7. **Verify in one batched round**: deterministic tell scan (impeccable-style + Krebs-style), craft-floor measurements, screenshots at 320/375/768/1280×800/1440, keyboard and reduced-motion passes, LLM critique isolated from detector output; optional AIM/UIClip regression signals.
8. **Fix in one batch, re-verify once, stop** (impeccable's bounded passes; Duan et al.'s diminishing returns).
9. **Remember**: log the direction, faces, palette and macrostructure used so the next project differs (Anthropic's advice to keep notes of what was tried; Hallmark log).
10. **Validate with people** when stakes justify it: desirability test with product reaction cards against the intended attributes — pre-register which words count as on-brand and report the share of participants choosing ≥1 of them in their top 5, from a ~25-word list with ~40% negative terms (NN/g, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/microsoft-desirability-toolkit/; Benedek & Miner 2002) [EMP method]; first-impression (5-second) tests; A/B tests for conversion (covered by other research notes).

## 5. Cross-source synthesis

### 5.1 Consensus (≥3 independent sources agree)

1. **Slop is the absence of decisions, not a style.** Every serious source says the tell is the *unchosen* default, and that any listed pattern is legitimate when the brief or the design system asks for it (Anthropic v2/v3, impeccable, gstack, signs-of-ai-design, avoid-ai-design, unslop-ui, Krebs). The evaluator must therefore read the brief/DESIGN.md before judging.
2. **Ground in the subject, plan before code, review the plan for genericness.** Anthropic (token plan → review → build), impeccable (direction contract, concept-seed), OpenAI (visual thesis, content plan, interaction thesis before building), Hallmark (pre-flight + pre-emit critique), taste-skill (design read + dials).
3. **Restraint with one signature.** One boldness / one authored motion moment / one accent by default / decoration must have a job (Anthropic, OpenAI, impeccable, gstack, ui-skills, Hallmark).
4. **The recurring tells** named by ≥3 sources (and, where available, measured by Krebs or the Reddit study): indigo/purple gradients and CTAs; Inter/system/Geist as the only or display face and the "tasteful free font" cluster; three identical icon-topped cards; coloured side-stripe borders; gradient text; tracked caps eyebrows and hero pill badges; the centred hero formula; cards as the default container, nested cards, one radius and one grey shadow everywhere; glows/halos on dark; the cream + serif + terracotta second-order look; emoji as icons; invented stats, testimonials and logos; the same fade-up on every section; bounce easing; buzzword and aphoristic copy.
5. **A shared craft floor**: WCAG AA contrast, 45–75ch measure, ~16 px body, ~1.5 body leading, a 4/8-based spacing scale, more space above headings than below, animate transform/opacity only with reduced-motion paths and ≤300 ms UI timings, visible focus, complete component states, no `transition: all` (Vercel, impeccable, gstack, Hallmark, ui-skills, Kowalski, platform systems).
6. **Verify by rendering, deterministically where possible, in bounded passes** (impeccable, gstack, Hallmark, OpenAI's Playwright loop, Anthropic's screenshots); deterministic DOM checks reach ~5–10% false positives in Krebs' QA.
7. **Honesty**: no fabricated claims, people, logos or chrome (Hallmark, impeccable, gstack, signs-of-ai-design).

### 5.3 Gaps (not answered by the sources read)

- **Validation**: apart from Krebs' 192 labels and the Reddit perception study, no tell has measured precision/recall or a measured effect on trust, conversion or task success. The only controlled study of AI-generated UIs found (n=62, 12 stimuli) is small and screenshot-based.
- **CJK/RTL craft**: almost every typographic rule here is Latin-centric; WCAG's 40-character CJK measure is the only number found. A dedicated note on Chinese/Japanese layout (W3C clreq/jlreq, CJK font availability and licensing, default sizes and leading) is needed — important given the project owner's audience.
- **Data visualisation craft**: only fragments (tabular numerals, colour-blind-safe palettes, no gradient metrics). Needs its own research note.
- **Native platforms**: impeccable's `ios.md`/`android.md` (derived from ehmo/platform-design-skills, MIT) were not read in depth; HIG/Material numbers used here cover type, targets and motion only.
- **Motion evidence**: almost entirely practitioner guidance; NN/g's durations are the closest to research.
- **Model-specific priors drift** with every model release; no source measures them systematically.
- **LLM-as-judge reliability for "looks generated"** is untested; UIClip's baselines (incl. GPT-4V) were near chance at choosing better designs.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Self-calibrating "default probing".** Instead of (or in addition to) a hand-maintained saturated-looks list, should UI-Evaluator sample the agent's own *unguided* output for the bare brief (e.g. 5 generations) and treat that cluster as the rut to avoid for this model and brief? It would track model drift automatically (§2.3), but cost and validity are unknown.
2. **Thresholds** marked [HEUR] (cross-output similarity, accent area, ghost-card blur, near-black/acid-accent hue buckets) need a labelled corpus. Who labels, and with what agreement target given α≈0.37 among designers in UIClip's data?
3. **CJK and Chinese-market conventions.** Do Chinese users read the same tells (purple gradients, cards, eyebrows) as "AI"? Chinese commerce and super-app UIs are legitimately denser; which density and typography rules change for CJK? Needs its own research note.
4. **Where is the "most advanced, yet acceptable" optimum for UI?** Prototypicality raises first impressions (Tuch 2012) while distinctiveness is the user's explicit goal; the right balance per surface mode is untested.
5. **Third-order slop.** avoid-ai-design predicts a handmade "anti-AI" wave (grain, hand-drawn type, collage). How often should statuses be reviewed — per model release, quarterly? Who owns the catalogue?
6. **Reuse vs. reimplementation of detectors.** impeccable's engine is Apache-2.0 (NOTICE and change notes required on derivation, as gstack does); Krebs' code is MIT; signs-of-ai-design text is CC BY-SA 4.0 (share-alike if copied). Do we wrap the installed impeccable engine (gstack's approach), port rules, or write our own?
7. **LLM judges and "looks generated".** No benchmark measures whether multimodal LLMs can tell generic-but-competent from distinctive designs; UIClip's baselines were near chance at picking the better design. Build a small benchmark before relying on an LLM verdict.
8. **Outcome evidence.** Does removing tells change conversion, trust or task success? Only the 2026 MDPI study (n=62, 12 static stimuli) touches this. A/B tests on real products are needed (coordinate with the empirical-evaluation note).
9. **Operate surfaces.** Many tells (Inter, cards, centred layouts) are often *correct* in app UI. Should detectors be mode-aware by default, and how is the mode inferred automatically from a route?
10. **Motion auditing.** Can durations, easings and per-frequency budgets be extracted reliably at runtime (Web Animations API, computed transitions) to automate CRAFT-048–052?
11. **AIM maintenance.** The codebase is dormant (last commit 2023-06-11, Python 3.7). Which metrics are worth porting, judged by correlation with our own labels?
