# 04 — Evaluation skills, research prototypes, and user-feedback intake

- **Date:** 2026-10-01
- **Author:** research agent (Claude Code), for the UI-Evaluator project
- **ID prefix for adopt items:** `EVAL-`
- **Status:** Complete (first full pass). Section 4 is the adopt list; section 5 lists what still needs checking.

**Executive summary.** (1) No existing skill implements Nielsen's actual heuristic-evaluation protocol (≥ 2 passes, 3–5 independent evaluators, severity rated afterwards by ≥ 3 raters and averaged, debrief); most run one agent in one pass and assign severity while detecting. `emiliacurie/arely-skills` is the only one that truly isolates parallel evaluators and reports disagreement; `uw-ssec`'s plugin specifies merge (dedupe + mean) and calibration (resolve > 1-point gaps) in text. (2) The decisive quality lever is **evidence gating**: the only production system that measured itself (`noodisD/UXAgent`) found about 2 of 8 raw agent findings real and cut 98 raw findings to 28 with deterministic + verifier gates (trace-resolved evidence, harness-failure filter, scope filter, skeptical claim verifier, evidence-capped confidence). (3) `carlsz/ux-agent-skills` (report contract, render-vs-source honesty, journey replay with vacuous-pass defences) and `averliz/visual-ux-review-toolkit` (driver-conformance gate, naive-user live walkthrough, measured layout checks, measured-only annotation boxes) are the best engineering bases; `Jackwwj619/UXBench` gives the best benchmark design (coverage-gated exploration, evidence-capped scores, repair-lift Δ, anchors + re-skinned siblings). (4) Feedback tools (Agentation, Marker.io, Formbricks, PostHog, MarkuprPlus) supply good intake schemas and triage rules, but none links feedback to heuristic findings — that is UI-Evaluator's opening. (5) Several sources contain verifiable errors (WCAG 4.1.1 kept in a "2.2" audit; a ~75% problem-yield figure that belongs to ~five aggregated evaluators, quoted in a single-agent skill; an A/B sample-size example off by ~6%; an incomplete severity lookup table with relabelled factors; a paraphrased, unscored "SUS"); holistic quality ratings in UICrit show near-zero inter-rater agreement, so single UX scores should not be a primary output.

## 1. Scope and method

Scope: (a) agent skills that operationalise textbook HCI evaluation (Nielsen heuristic evaluation, cognitive walkthrough, Norman principles, WCAG audits, usability-test planning), (b) research prototypes and benchmarks for LLM-based usability evaluation, and (c) tools for ingesting real user feedback (annotations, bug reports, surveys, product analytics) into an agent loop.

Method: for every repository the full file tree was listed (via `gh api …/git/trees/HEAD?recursive=1` or a shallow clone into the session scratchpad; sparse checkouts for Formbricks and PostHog) and the skill/agent/prompt/schema/script files were read in full, not only READMEs; data files were parsed with Python (UICrit CSV, MLLM-Usability study CSVs, arely's XLSX template); numeric claims in sources were re-computed where possible (A/B sample size, peeking simulation, severity-table coverage, inter-rater reliability). Papers were read from arXiv abstract/HTML pages and, for UXBench and Spencer 2000, from downloaded PDFs. Primary HCI sources (NN/g, W3C, MeasuringU, Brooke, Spencer/Wharton, Hertzum & Jacobsen) were re-checked and are summarised in §2.0. Every concrete rule or number carries a URL and, for repo content, a file path. Anything not verified is marked **[unverified]**; my own computations are labelled as such. Text from sources is paraphrased; quotes are kept under 15 words.

## 2. Source-by-source notes

### 2.0 Canonical baselines used to check every source

Before judging the skills, the primary sources they claim to implement were re-checked, because several skills misquote them.

- **Severity (Nielsen 1994).** Severity is a combination of *frequency*, *impact* and *persistence*, plus an overall *market impact* judgement; scale 0 = not a problem, 1 = cosmetic, 2 = minor, 3 = major, 4 = catastrophe (must be fixed before the product is released). Ratings should be collected *after* the evaluation sessions (e.g., by questionnaire on the merged problem list), a single evaluator's severity ratings are "too unreliable to be trusted", and the practical recommendation is to use the mean of three evaluators' ratings. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/
- **Number of evaluators and passes (Nielsen 1994, archived original).** Averaged over six projects, a single evaluator found only 35% of known problems; Nielsen recommends "about five evaluators, but certainly at least three". Problems found by *i* independent evaluators follow `ProblemsFound(i) = N · (1 − (1 − λ)^i)` (Nielsen & Landauer 1993), with λ (one evaluator's share) ranging 19–51%, mean 34%, and N ranging 16–50, mean 33. Evaluators go through the interface at least twice (first for flow/scope, second for specific elements), sessions last one to two hours, evaluators must not communicate until all evaluations are done, each problem is listed *separately* with reference to the violated heuristic (so a dialogue element with three problems gets three listings), and an optional debriefing with evaluators, observer and designers follows. One case study had a benefit/cost ratio of 48. Source: archived https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/ (fetched via `curl`, text extracted). The 2023 rewrite (Moran & Gordon) keeps 3–5 independent evaluators, two passes, 1–2 h time-box, and adds affinity-diagram synthesis: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/ . The often-cited figure of about 75% for five evaluators comes from reading the article's Figure 2 curve; the text itself does not state it.
- **WCAG 2.2.** 4.1.1 Parsing is "Obsolete and removed" in WCAG 2.2; the nine new criteria are 2.4.11 (AA), 2.4.12 (AAA), 2.4.13 (AAA), 2.5.7 (AA), 2.5.8 (AA), 3.2.6 (A), 3.3.7 (A), 3.3.8 (AA), 3.3.9 (AAA). Source: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#parsing
- **Cognitive walkthrough questions (Wharton, Rieman, Lewis & Polson 1994), as listed in Spencer's CHI 2000 paper (Table 1)** — paraphrased here: (1) will the user try to achieve the right effect; (2) will the user notice that the correct action is available; (3) will the user associate that action with the effect they are trying to achieve; (4) after performing the correct action, will the user see that progress is being made toward the task. For each step the team either tells a credible success story or records that none can be told. **Spencer's streamlined CW** reduces this to two questions — will the user know what to do at this step, and, having done the right thing, will they know it was right and that they are progressing toward the goal — records nothing when both stories are plausible, and otherwise records possible learnability problems, design ideas, design gaps and task-analysis problems; ground rules: no designing, no defending a design, no debating cognitive theory, the usability specialist leads; findings are phrased tentatively as potential problems. Source: Spencer, *The Streamlined Cognitive Walkthrough Method*, CHI 2000, pp. 353–359, PDF at http://facweb.cs.depaul.edu/cmiller/eval/p353-spencer.pdf (downloaded and text-extracted).
- **Evaluator effect (Hertzum & Jacobsen 2001, IJHCI):** across 11 studies of CW, HE and think-aloud, the average agreement between any two evaluators using the same method on the same system ranged **from 5% to 65%**, for novices and experts, cosmetic and severe problems, detection and severity rating alike (abstract via https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/the-evaluator-effect-a-chilling-fact-about-usability-evaluation-methods(b6e91bbb-fd0b-435a-80f3-a4844fdde6db).html). This is the empirical reason for independent multi-evaluator runs and post-hoc severity averaging.
- **Five users (Nielsen 2000):** with a typical per-user discovery rate L = 31%, problems found by *n* users ≈ N·(1 − (1 − L)^n), so five users find ≈ 85%; Nielsen recommends three iterative rounds of five rather than one round of fifteen, 3–4 users per group when there are two user groups, ~20 users for quantitative studies, ~15 for card sorting. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/ (the fetched summary garbled the formula; the form above is the standard Nielsen–Landauer model, consistent with the 1994 article quoted earlier).

These baselines are used below to flag errors in individual skills.

### 2.1 mastepanoski/claude-skills (nielsen-heuristics-audit, cognitive-walkthrough, don-norman-principles-audit, wcag-accessibility-audit)

- **What it is:** A collection of 12 single-file Agent-Skills (SKILL.md, open standard) covering UX evaluation (Nielsen, CW, Norman, WCAG, UI design review, IxDF-based `ux-audit-rethink`) and AI governance (NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, OWASP LLM Top 10, GDPR). 54 stars, last push 2026-06-05.
- **URL:** https://github.com/mastepanoski/claude-skills
- **License:** MIT (copyright 2026 mastepanoski) — text can be adapted with attribution and the license notice.
- **Files read (in full):** `skills/nielsen-heuristics-audit/SKILL.md` (510 lines), `skills/cognitive-walkthrough/SKILL.md` (563), `skills/don-norman-principles-audit/SKILL.md` (227), `skills/wcag-accessibility-audit/SKILL.md` (787); skimmed structure of `skills/ux-audit-rethink/SKILL.md` (1,126) and `skills/ui-design-review/SKILL.md` (1,059); `CLAUDE.md`, `scripts/validate.js`.

**Key content (own words).**
- *Nielsen audit* (`skills/nielsen-heuristics-audit/SKILL.md`): inputs are `interface_description` (required) plus optional `screenshots_or_links`, `user_flows`, `known_issues`, `competitive_context`. For each heuristic it lists items to check and common violations. Procedure: preparation (identify 5–7 key tasks if none given) → heuristic-by-heuristic pass → cross-heuristic analysis (patterns, root causes, prioritising by severity, frequency and impact on critical tasks) → report. Per-finding fields: location, description, screenshot/reference, affected tasks, severity 0–4, 2–3 recommendations; plus positive examples. Report: executive summary with counts per severity, top 3 issues, an overall usability score out of 10, per-heuristic compliance shown as 1–5 stars, prioritized action items with Effort (Low/Medium/High), quick wins, long-term items, methodology/limitations section.
- *Cognitive walkthrough* (`skills/cognitive-walkthrough/SKILL.md`): required inputs `task_description`, `user_persona` (experience level, domain knowledge, technical proficiency, device context, motivation), `interface_description`; optional starting point and success criteria. The task is decomposed into atomic actions, each being one observable user behaviour; each action is evaluated with the Wharton et al. four questions, each rated ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌; then failure points are classed as critical blockers / major friction / minor issues / cognitive load.
- *Norman audit* (`skills/don-norman-principles-audit/SKILL.md`): seven principles (discoverability, affordance, signifiers, feedback, mapping, constraints, conceptual model), severity in a 4-level word scale (Catastrophic/High/Medium/Low), overall 1–10 score.
- *WCAG audit* (`skills/wcag-accessibility-audit/SKILL.md`): POUR structure, defaults to WCAG 2.2 AA, automated pass (axe, WAVE, Lighthouse) then manual checklists (keyboard 15 min, screen reader 20 min, zoom/reflow/contrast, forms, semantics, ARIA), PASS/FAIL per success criterion, priority P0 = any Level A failure … P4 = AAA, and a reporting template.
- *Cross-cutting:* every skill has a Security Notice section treating screenshots, links, bug reports and user complaints as untrusted content (prompt-injection defence, OWASP LLM01), which is directly relevant to our feedback-intake design.

**Strengths.** Clean, portable SKILL.md layout; explicit inputs with REQUIRED/OPTIONAL tags; faithful 0–4 Nielsen scale and Wharton four questions; repeated caveat that the audit is a simulation to be validated with real users; prompt-injection handling of user-supplied feedback.

**Weaknesses / errors (verified against primary sources).**
1. Single-pass, single-evaluator design; nothing about ≥2 passes, independent evaluators, or merging — contrary to Nielsen's procedure above.
2. The Nielsen skill says heuristic evaluation "finds ~75% of issues"; per Nielsen this is the yield of ~five aggregated evaluators, while one evaluator averages 35% (archived NN/g article above). An agent running this skill once is the single-evaluator case.
3. Severity is assigned *during* the pass by the same evaluator; Nielsen recommends collecting it afterwards and averaging three raters.
4. Fabricated precision: the CW report template asks for an estimated first-attempt success percentage, per-persona success rates and time-on-task, and a predicted success-rate gain per fix; the WCAG template shows example tool issue counts and a Lighthouse score. Without measurement these numbers are invented — we should forbid them.
5. WCAG content errors: the skill claims WCAG 2.2 but keeps 4.1.1 Parsing as a Level A check (removed in 2.2, see §2.0); it attaches a 2 px minimum, high-contrast focus requirement to 2.4.7 (that wording belongs to the AAA criterion 2.4.13); it puts the 1280 px/400% reflow condition under 1.4.4 Resize Text.
6. Star ratings and out-of-10 or out-of-100 scores with no defined rubric — not reproducible.

**Adopt.** The Security Notice pattern for untrusted feedback; the required-input contract for CW (task, persona, start state, success criteria); atomic action = one observable behaviour; the PASS/FAIL-per-criterion WCAG layout and the rule that any Level A failure is P0; the closing limitations section. **Do not adopt:** predicted success percentages, uncalibrated star scores, the 75% claim, 4.1.1 Parsing.

Skills are mirrored in `.claude/skills/` and `.agents/skills/` (the latter adds `agents/openai.yaml` for Codex with `allow_implicit_invocation: false`). 18 stars, last push 2026-04-11.

- **License:** MIT (copyright holder: hci-review-skill contributors) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** all 10 core `.claude/skills/*/SKILL.md` and `prototype-hci-pack` in full; 4 of the 14 research/prototyping skills in full and the other 10 verified identical by diff; `heuristic-eval-beads` and the Beads phase of `prototype-hci-pack-beads` in full, and the MAJOR/MINOR mapping section of all other `-beads` variants (their Phase 1 is the core skill text); all 18 `docs/hci/templates/*.md`; `README.md`; `.agents/skills/heuristic-eval/agents/openai.yaml`; diffed `.claude` vs `.agents` mirrors (only frontmatter differs: `argument-hint`, `disable-model-invocation`). **Key content.**
- *heuristic-eval* (`.claude/skills/heuristic-eval/SKILL.md`): every issue must be tied to a concrete part of the system; required table `ID | Heuristic | Screen/flow/component | Evidence | Why it matters | Severity (0-4) | Recommendation`; severity 0–4, with 4 defined as severe or likely to block or mislead users; synthesis = top 5 by severity, quickest wins, structural issues needing redesign vs. visual tweaks. Dedup rule across skills: if a consistency-audit artifact exists, heuristic #4 only covers findings not already there.
- *cognitive-walkthrough*: the agent role-plays a plausible first-time user who only has the cues visible in the interface; per step records step number, user goal, **visible cues**, **likely action**, Q1–Q4 answers, breakdowns, recommendation. The four questions are paraphrased (Q3 becomes whether the user understands that the action leads toward the goal; Q4 whether the feedback makes sense after acting). Extra checks: hidden prerequisites, unclear affordances, weak/missing feedback, misleading labels, dead ends, unclear recovery path. Template `docs/hci/templates/cognitive-walkthrough.md` gives the step-log table.
- *failure-path-audit* (119 lines, the most original skill): eight categories, each with a fixed table — empty states (rated OK / PARTIAL / MISSING), loading (indicator, location, blocks interaction?, timeout handling; asks what happens at >5 s and >30 s and whether it can be cancelled), validation errors (rules, when validation runs, message, placement, recovery; inline vs summary; on blur vs on submit; data preserved), permission failures, network failures (500, timeout, retry, data preserved, offline), partial completion (abandon/resume/autosave; bulk partial success), undo/cancel/recovery (soft vs hard delete), concurrent/conflict states. Quality bar: "Every happy path implies at least 3-5 failure paths." — and fewer found means look harder.
- *state-model*: per entity, tables of states (meaning, user-visible cue, entry/exit), allowed transitions (trigger, actor, side effects), invalid transitions, ambiguous states; UI-state coverage table `Entity | State | Has visual cue | Has available actions | Has transition feedback | Gap`; distinguishes backend state from user-perceived state.
- *consistency-audit*: 12 invariant areas (navigation, titles, layout rhythm, primary/secondary/destructive action treatment, form order & validation style, status badges, empty/loading/error/success states, search/filter/sort, modal/drawer/popover/toast usage, back/cancel/close semantics, icon meaning, terminology); table `Area | Expected invariant | Observed variants | Risk | Recommendation`; deliberate variation must be distinguished from accidental inconsistency.
- *vocabulary-audit*, *information-architecture* (sitemap, nav model, grouping, label audit, whether each action sits near the objects it acts on, depth/breadth, orphan screens), *conceptual-model* (actors/objects/actions/states/rules), *journey-map* (3–7 journeys; first-time, core, edit/undo, failure recovery, returning user).
- *hci-scorecard* template: eight dimensions rated 1–5 (clarity of purpose within 10 seconds, navigation, terminology, state visibility, error prevention/recovery, recognition over recall, cross-screen consistency, failure-path coverage), total /40 with bands 33–40 (ready for user testing), 25–32, 17–24, 8–16, plus an iteration-over-iteration comparison table.
- *Beads variants*: run the core skill, then if the `bd` CLI exists create one parent issue and child issues per finding: severity 3–4 → `[MAJOR]` priority 0 (always), severity 1–2 → `[MINOR]` priority 2 (only with `--include-minor`); CW mapping: Q1/Q2 failures → MAJOR, Q3/Q4 → MINOR; each child gets `--description` (what/why + evidence), `--notes` (location), `--acceptance` (fix or verification), linked to the parent with `bd dep add`. Graceful skip if `bd` is missing.
- *The 14 research/prototyping skills* (representative-task-writer, usability-test-planner, satisfaction-questionnaire-writer, error-and-time-metrics-analyzer, persona-synthesizer, task-analysis-writer, etc.) are **identical boilerplate**: a diff after removing name/purpose/handoff lines returns zero differences for all 14. The only skill-specific content is a one-line purpose plus 5–8-line templates, e.g. representative task = user goal, scenario context, starting state, success condition, **avoided hints**; usability test plan = objective, participants, tasks, metrics, moderator prompts, evidence capture, risks/confounds; post-task questionnaire = ease/confidence/clarity on 1–5 scales plus frustration points. The shared body does contain one useful rule set: separate observed facts, inferred assumptions and speculative hypotheses; a hypothesised persona is not evidence.

**Strengths.** Treats the UI as a system (state models, invariants, vocabulary) rather than screen-by-screen; codebase-anchored evidence; failure-path taxonomy is concrete and testable; explicit cross-skill dedup; finding → tracker-issue conversion with acceptance criteria; iteration comparison in the scorecard.

**Weaknesses.** Single evaluator, single pass; severity 4 wording drifts from Nielsen's (fix before release); CW Q-wording is paraphrased; research-planning skills are empty shells (no SUS/SEQ, no sample-size guidance, no metric definitions, no analysis method); scorecard bands are uncalibrated; no screenshot capture or live-browser interaction.

**Adopt.** Failure-path category list and its tables; UI-state coverage table; consistency-invariant list; `visible cues` and `likely action` columns in the CW step log; cross-skill dedup rule; finding→issue conversion with `acceptance` = verification step; representative-task `avoided hints` field; fact/inference/hypothesis separation.

### 2.3 uw-ssec/rse-plugins — `plugins/uiux-design-team`

- **What it is:** A Claude Code plugin simulating a ten-agent design team (ux-design-lead orchestrator, ux-researcher, accessibility-specialist, visual-designer, interaction-designer, ux-writer, motion-designer, IA, design-systems-engineer, design-ops), 10 commands and ~25 skills with progressive-disclosure `references/`. 136 files in the plugin.
- **URL:** https://github.com/uw-ssec/rse-plugins/tree/main/plugins/uiux-design-team
- **License — conflict:** the repository `LICENSE` is BSD-3-Clause (UW Scientific Software Engineering Center), but this plugin's `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` declares `"license": "proprietary"` and `plugins/uiux-design-team/README.md` (§License) names BitBarrel LLC as proprietary owner. All other plugins in the repo declare MIT or BSD-3-Clause. Until clarified, treat this plugin as **ideas only — do not copy text**.
- **Files read:** `skills/usability-evaluation/SKILL.md`, `references/scoring-methods.md` and `references/walkthrough-guide.md` in full, the per-heuristic severity guidance in `references/nielsen-heuristics.md`, `commands/evaluate-usability.md`, `commands/design-review.md`, `skills/ab-testing-strategy/SKILL.md`, the synthesis section of `agents/ux-design-lead.md` (885 lines), research-sampling sections of `agents/ux-researcher.md`, `plugin.json`, plugin README.

**Key content.**
- *Heuristic-evaluation workflow* (`skills/usability-evaluation/SKILL.md`): 3–5 evaluators (diminishing returns past 5) → each evaluates independently, recording screen, heuristic, description, severity, fix → **merge: dedupe and average severity** → **calibrate: evaluators review each other's ratings and resolve disagreements of more than 1 point; target inter-rater agreement within ±1** → sort by severity, deliver with screenshots and fixes. Release gates: every severity-4 issue has an owner and a fix in this release; no severity-3 issue may be marked won't-fix without explicit product approval.
- *Finding template* (mandated format): `ID` (HE-nnn), `Screen`, `Component`, `State` (e.g., empty/first-load), `Heuristic violated` (may be several, e.g., H5 + H9), `Description` (with screenshot reference), `Severity` plus separate `Frequency`, `Impact`, `Persistence` lines, numbered `Recommended fix` steps (example includes concrete attributes such as `inputmode="numeric"` and validate-on-blur), `Effort` (S/M/L) and `Owner`.
- *Per-heuristic severity anchors* (`references/nielsen-heuristics.md`): each heuristic ends with consequence-based ranges, e.g., H1 violations are 3–4 when users cannot tell whether a critical action (payment, submission) succeeded and 1–2 for non-critical status; H3 is 4 when users lose data or cannot reverse a critical action; H4 is 1 when inconsistency is purely aesthetic. Useful as rater anchors.
- *Severity lookup matrix* (`commands/evaluate-usability.md`): frequency (rare/occasional/frequent) × persistence (one-time/recurring) as rows, impact (easy/moderate/hard to overcome) as columns, yielding 1–4. Example rows: rare+one-time → 1/2/3; frequent+recurring → 3/4/4.
- *Cognitive walkthrough* (`references/walkthrough-guide.md`): grounded in CE+ exploratory-learning theory; preparation = user profile → task → **correct action sequence, one user action per step** → materials; four Wharton questions with success indicators and failure examples; **success and failure stories** (narratives of the user's reasoning, presented as the method's most valuable output); issue log `ID | Step | Question | Issue | Severity`; 1–3 evaluators sufficient for CW vs 3–5 for HE; pluralistic-walkthrough variant (4–8 people, everyone writes their action *before* discussion). Its claim that feedback should arrive within 100–400 ms is not traced to a source [unverified; Nielsen's response-time limits are 0.1 s / 1 s / 10 s].
- *Multi-specialist review* (`commands/design-review.md`): lead routes the artifact to accessibility, visual, interaction and UX-writing specialists; each finding records `Specialist | Category | Location | Issue | Impact | Recommendation`; final three-level readiness verdict (ready for handoff, needs revision, needs significant rework). No dedup or disagreement handling is specified for this path.
- *Measurement references* (`references/scoring-methods.md`): SUS procedure, SEQ, NASA-TLX (raw and weighted), SUPR-Q, NPS, completion rate (binary or partial credit), time on task (report the **median**, task times are right-skewed), error rate; questionnaire timing (SEQ after each task, SUS after all tasks, before debrief); SUS comparisons with non-parametric tests and confidence intervals.
- *A/B testing* (`skills/ab-testing-strategy/SKILL.md`): hypothesis template linking a change to a metric, an expected direction and size, and an evidence-based reason, with a four-item checkpoint (specific, measurable, grounded, falsifiable); one primary metric plus 1–2 guardrails plus counter-metrics; verify instrumentation before launch; two-proportion sample-size function; minimum one business cycle (7–14 days); no peeking; ship rule = p < 0.05 and CI excludes 0 and no guardrail degraded; check segments for cancelling effects.

**Errors found (checked).**
- The A/B example comment says the sample-size function returns 7,663 per variant for baseline 5% → 6%; running the function exactly as written returns **8,158** (verified locally with SciPy 1.14.1 and with Python's `statistics.NormalDist`).
- "Peeking ten times inflates the false-positive rate to ~40%": a local simulation of 10 equally spaced looks at α = 0.05 gives ≈19%, matching the classic repeated-significance-testing result (Armitage et al., 1969) [the 19% figure is from my simulation; the 1969 table is cited from memory — verify].
- SUS grade table (90–100 = A+, 80–89 = A, …) does not match MeasuringU's curved grading scale, where 68 is a C and an A requires above 80.3 (https://measuringu.com/sus/). Average SUS 68 across 500 studies is correct (same URL). SEQ average lies between about 5.3 and 5.6 across 400+ tasks (https://measuringu.com/seq10/), so 5.5 is fine; average completion rate 78% across ~1,200 tasks is correct (https://measuringu.com/task-completion/). The efficiency-ratio cut-offs (0.7/0.4) and error-rate targets (<5%, >15%) have no source [unverified].
- `commands/design-review.md` uses an **inverted** 1–4 scale (1 = Critical, 4 = Enhancement), contradicting the plugin's own 0–4 Nielsen scale, and cites a 44×44 px minimum target size, which is WCAG 2.5.5 (AAA); the AA criterion 2.5.8 is 24×24 CSS px (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

**Strengths.** The only skill in this survey that specifies merge (dedupe + average) **and** calibration (±1 disagreement threshold) for multiple evaluators; strong finding template with separate frequency/impact/persistence; CW success/failure stories; A/B guardrails and the verify-instrumentation-first rule.

**Weaknesses.** Proprietary-license ambiguity; internal scale inconsistency; several unsourced numeric benchmarks; specialists in design-review are not independent evaluators of the same heuristics, so no inter-rater logic applies there.

**Adopt (ideas, rewritten in our words).** Merge = dedupe + mean severity; calibrate any item whose ratings differ by >1; release gates for severity 3/4; finding template with frequency/impact/persistence fields and `State`; CW failure-story narrative; A/B hypothesis template + guardrails + instrumentation check + fixed horizon.

### 2.4 EliaAlberti/ux-audit-skill

- **What it is:** A single Agent-Skill (`SKILL.md`, 266 lines) for screenshot-based heuristic audits of a journey, plus a Pillow script that draws severity-coloured boxes on screenshots, and a worked example (a fictional sign-up flow). 19 stars, last push 2026-06-12.
- **URL:** https://github.com/EliaAlberti/ux-audit-skill
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Elia Alberti) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** `SKILL.md` (in full), `scripts/annotate.py` (73 lines, in full), `examples/annotations.json`, `examples/report.md` (front-matter, summary, findings overview and first finding).

**Key content.**
- *Intake gate:* ask product + user goal, platform, user type (first-time/returning), depth (full sweep or 2–3 frameworks), journey order; the audit must not start until the user goal is known, because severity is anchored to that goal.
- *Four passes:* **A — goal walk** as the stated user (anchors severity: a violation that does not affect the goal should rarely exceed severity 2); **B — framework sweep** with a mandatory citation format (`Nielsen #1`, `WCAG 1.4.3`, `B&S: Guidance/Feedback`); **C — named recurring checks**; **D — journey level** (cross-screen consistency, progress visibility, memory load across steps, Peak-End on worst moment and final screen, Fogg B=MAP on the key conversion action).
- *Calibration rules:* merge duplicates (same issue on several screens → one journey-level finding listing occurrences); IDs `F-01…` sorted by severity, positives `P-01…`; include 2–5 positives; "prefer 8 strong, evidenced findings over 25 padded ones"; a framework that surfaces nothing gets no findings.
- *Evidence rules (declared non-negotiable):* every finding must point at something visible in a named screenshot; interaction qualities (animation, focus order, latency, keyboard, screen reader) go to a separate list of things that cannot be assessed from static screens; never invent illegible text; severity 3–4 requires both a heuristic citation and visible evidence; borderline contrast is flagged for checking with a contrast tool instead of asserting a ratio.
- *Severity:* Nielsen 0–4 relabelled 4 Critical / 3 Major / 2 Minor / 1 Cosmetic / ✓ Positive, rated by impact on the stated goal × number of users × repetition.
- *Named recurring checks with stable IDs* (16): CTA-AMBIGUITY, DEAD-END, FAKE-AFFORDANCE, CONTRAST-FAIL, TOUCH-TARGET, JARGON-LEAK, PROGRESS-BLIND, ERROR-VAGUE, FORM-FRICTION, OVERLOAD, PATTERN-DRIFT, TRUST-GAP, DARK-PATTERN, STATE-GAP, HIERARCHY-FLAT, RECALL-TAX — the table is extended only when the same unnamed issue shows up in two audits.
- *Frameworks (A–L):* Nielsen, Shneiderman's 8 rules, Gerhardt-Powals, Bastien & Scapin, Hick/Fitts/Miller/Jakob/Peak-End, Fogg, Cialdini (with misuse → DARK-PATTERN sev 3+), Gestalt, Norman, Tognazzini, a static-checkable WCAG 2.1 subset, and a 10-item content-design heuristic list.
- *Annotation pipeline:* `annotations.json` = list of `{image, out, marks:[{id, sev, x, y, w, h}]}` with the box as **fractions (0–1) of image width/height**; colours 4 red `#D32F2F`, 3 orange `#F57C00`, 2 amber `#F9A825`, 1 blue `#1976D2`, 0/positive green `#2E7D32`; label tag placed above the box (or below if no room). Workflow requires viewing each annotated image and fixing coordinates once if a marker is on the wrong element; HTML pin fallback if Pillow is unavailable.
- *Report:* YAML front-matter with counts per severity; executive summary written for a stakeholder who reads nothing else; scope & method incl. the not-assessable list; findings overview table `ID | Sev | Screen | Check | Finding | Heuristics`; per-finding `Check`, `Heuristics`, `Evidence`, `Impact on goal`, `Recommendation`, `Effort S/M/L`; journey-level findings; what works; prioritized recommendations (quick wins = sev 3–4 & effort S); **framework coverage table** (framework → finding IDs, or none found); sources.

**Strengths.** Best evidence discipline of the static-screenshot skills (the explicit not-assessable list prevents hallucinated interaction claims); severity anchored to a declared user goal; stable check IDs make audits comparable over time and across runs (useful for regression tests of our skill); clean, reusable annotation format.

**Weaknesses.** Static screenshots only; single evaluator; bounding boxes are model-estimated fractions (approximate by design); WCAG 2.1 not 2.2, and its target-size line mixes 2.5.5 (AAA, 44 px) with the 24 px floor that belongs to 2.5.8 (AA, WCAG 2.2); breadth of 12 frameworks risks citation-shopping.

**Adopt.** Goal-anchored severity + intake gate; evidence rules incl. the not-assessable-from-static-screens list; fewer, stronger findings over padding, and no finding for a framework that surfaces nothing; stable named-check IDs (extend with our own); fractional-bbox annotation JSON + colour scale + visual re-check; framework-coverage table.

### 2.5 emiliacurie/arely-skills — `ux-audit-panel` (+ `design-critique`)

- **What it is:** Two Claude Code skills by Arely Curiel (Telos Labs). `ux-audit-panel` runs 2–5 *independent evaluator subagents* over the same 49-statement checklist and merges them with a script; `design-critique` role-plays a four-designer visual-craft panel in one pass. 2 stars, last push 2026-09-17.
- **URL:** https://github.com/emiliacurie/arely-skills
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Arely Curiel) — scripts and text adaptable with attribution. The bundled `assets/template.xlsx` statement bank appears to derive from an older community audit spreadsheet whose origin is not stated **[provenance unverified — do not copy the 49 statements]**.
- **Files read:** `skills/ux-audit-panel/SKILL.md`, `reference/perspectives.md`, `reference/rubric.md`, `reference/statements.md`, `scripts/merge.py` (99 lines), `scripts/write_audit.py` (168), `scripts/capture.sh`; inspected `assets/template.xlsx` formulas with openpyxl; `skills/design-critique/SKILL.md`, `references/critique-checklist.md` (Part A roster and start of Part B).

**Key content — the independent-panel protocol (`skills/ux-audit-panel/SKILL.md`).**
1. *Setup:* ask (one question at a time) for target (URL(s) or screenshot folder), number of evaluators (default 3, min 2, max 5), output folder, project details. Assign each evaluator a **distinct perspective** (never the same twice) from six briefs in `reference/perspectives.md`: A1 accessibility-first, A2 first-time-user advocate, A3 visual craft/design systems, A4 conversion & content, A5 interaction & error recovery, A6 information architecture. Perspectives change what an evaluator notices and how harshly it weighs a flaw, never which statements are scored.
2. *Evidence capture* (`scripts/capture.sh`, Playwright CLI, isolated session): per URL a desktop 1440×900 fold and full-page screenshot, a 390×844 mobile fold and full-page screenshot, the accessibility snapshot, and console output; `urls.tsv` maps screen numbers to URLs. The run folder is self-contained (`evidence/`, `raw/`, `reference/`).
3. *Parallel, isolated evaluation:* all evaluators launched in a single message; each prompt tells the evaluator it is the only one, forbids reading `raw/` or looking for other agents' output; orchestrator rules: never reveal how many others exist or what they found, never pass one output into another prompt, never run sequentially with earlier results in context; if an evaluator fails, relaunch with the same prompt — never patch its scores; with fewer than 2 evaluator files, stop (no partial audit); the orchestrator never writes scores itself.
4. *Rubric (`reference/rubric.md`):* each statement scored 0–4 (4 = fully met, with exceptions searched for and none found; 3 = met with one nameable exception; 2 = partially met/inconsistent; 1 = mostly not met; 0 = not met or working against the user) or `"N/A"`. Rules: any score ≤ 2 needs a note naming **screen and element**; use N/A honestly (guessing a middling score to fill a cell corrupts the average); **no clustering on the middle score**; missing evidence is not evidence of failure (uncaptured states are N/A, not low). Output JSON: `{perspective, scores:{sheet:{row:{score, note}}}, top_issues:[{title, where, why, severity: high|medium|low}]}` with 3–6 top issues.
5. *Merge (`scripts/merge.py`):* per statement, keep every evaluator's score and note (labelled by perspective, in its own voice); **divergence = max − min of numeric scores ≥ 2** (`SPLIT = 2`), sorted worst spread first, written to `divergence-report.md` with each evaluator's score and note; plus each evaluator's top issues. Missing rows abort the merge.
6. *Spreadsheet (`scripts/write_audit.py` + template formulas):* per-statement average `=ROUND(AVERAGE(numeric cells),2)`, N/A cells excluded; normalised as `average/4*5` then `*100/5` (i.e., percent of max); sheet grade = mean of statement percents; global = mean of the nine sheet grades; a statement N/A for everyone is removed from the grade, an all-N/A sheet is excluded with `IFERROR`.
7. *Synthesis:* ≤ 15 lines under a *What this means* heading: the sharpest disagreements and why, and issues flagged independently by several evaluators — "Agreement is what to fix. Disagreement is what to take to real users."

**`design-critique`** is the cheap counterpart: four lenses cast adaptively from an 8-voice roster, each giving 1–2 strengths and 3–4 findings tagged Blocker/Major/Minor/Polish with location and principle; then points of agreement (raised by 2+ voices = high confidence), points of disagreement (surfaced as decisions for the user), top 3 next steps. It explicitly role-plays the voices in one pass and forbids spawning subagents to save tokens — so it is *not* independent. Cost rule in the skill: image tokens ≈ width × height ÷ 750, so capture at ~1000 px wide and downscale before reading. **Correction:** Anthropic's current vision docs count one visual token per 28 × 28 px patch, i.e., ⌈width/28⌉ × ⌈height/28⌉ tokens, with per-model caps (standard tier: 1,568 px long edge / 1,568 tokens; high-resolution tier, Claude 4.7 and later: 2,576 px / 4,784 tokens; e.g., a 1000 × 1000 screenshot = 1,296 tokens) (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision). The ÷ 750 rule is an older approximation; the advice to downscale screenshots still holds.

**Weaknesses.** The 49-statement bank is weak content: sheet titles do not match their statements (the consistency sheet holds minimalism items and the aesthetic sheet holds consistency items), one statement is duplicated across sheets, several are opinion (e.g., using bright colours, big type and sounds to grab attention) or outdated thresholds (a 12 pt font floor, ~9 mm touch targets). Scores are satisfaction-style 0–4 compliance ratings, not Nielsen *severity*; there is no problem list deduplication across evaluators (only `top_issues` side by side); the averaged percentage is presented as a grade without calibration.

**Adopt.** The isolation protocol verbatim in spirit (parallel launch, no cross-visibility, relaunch-not-patch, orchestrator never scores, minimum two evaluators); distinct perspective per evaluator; N/A-not-zero and missing evidence ≠ failure; anti-clustering rule; divergence threshold (spread ≥ 2 on a 0–4 scale) with notes in each evaluator's voice; agreement → fix, disagreement → test with users; capture recipe (desktop + mobile fold/full + a11y snapshot + console).

### 2.6 averliz/visual-ux-review-toolkit (`ux-flow-walkthrough` + `visual-ux-review`)

- **What it is:** Two harness-agnostic skills by Jeremy Teo that drive a **live browser**: `ux-flow-walkthrough` is an automated naive-user cognitive walkthrough across screens; `visual-ux-review` is a measured per-screen review at five breakpoints with an annotated HTML/PDF report. Includes a CI check that the shared driver reference is byte-identical in both skills, and a pytest for report provenance. 1 star, last push 2026-06-24.
- **URL:** https://github.com/averliz/visual-ux-review-toolkit
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Jeremy Teo) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** `skills/ux-flow-walkthrough/SKILL.md` (165 lines), `references/walkthrough-method.md`, `skills/visual-ux-review/SKILL.md` (355), `references/report-schema.md`, `references/ux-heuristics.md`, `references/browser-tools.md`, `tests/test_report_provenance.py`, feedback/export section of `scripts/generate_report.py` (667 lines).

**Key content — `ux-flow-walkthrough`.**
- *Persona discipline:* a first-time user with zero context who reads labels literally, takes the **most obvious** action (tie-break: the control whose label most literally matches the goal verb; the rejected tie is logged as a discoverability finding), never opens dev tools or uses insider knowledge; if auto-logged-in as admin, still behaves as a newcomer.
- *Modes:* A goal-directed (default; stop on goal achieved, Blocker with no workaround, or **3 consecutive steps with no progress**); B free-explore; C full auto-explore with budget `MAX_STEPS` = 60 and `MAX_DEPTH` = 8, breadth-first, **state de-dup by signature = normalized URL + hash of visible interactive elements (roles + accessible names)**, every skip logged, adversarial probes (empty submit, junk input, Back mid-flow, refresh mid-flow, double submit, open-then-dismiss incl. focus restore, dead-end check, destructive path up to — never through — confirmation).
- *Per-step loop:* observe (accessibility snapshot first; screenshot only at decisive moments) → state the persona's current intent in first person → CW1 goal match, CW2 visibility, CW3 label/affordance match, CW4 feedback → act → record *saw / intended / did / expected / actually happened / friction (severity + failed CW question)*. Mapping guidance: CW1/CW2 failures usually Blocker/High, CW3 High/Medium, CW4 High/Medium. **First-click test:** if the newcomer's most likely first click leads away from the goal, that is High even if recoverable.
- *False-finding controls:* **automation/driver timeouts are never reported as findings** — after any click/navigation error, re-observe before judging, and flag only if the *app* visibly failed to respond; **data-changing goals must be verified by persistence** — reload/re-read; a confirmation with no real change is High.
- *Severity:* Blocker / High / Medium / Low via a decision tree (can complete? → would a typical first-timer give up? → did they hesitate?).
- *Report:* outcome COMPLETED / COMPLETED-WITH-FRICTION / BLOCKED / ABANDONED; verdict on whether a naive first-timer would complete it (`WOULD` / `WOULD-WITH-EFFORT` / `WOULD-NOT`); step-by-step journey; findings with step, CW failure, newcomer experience, evidence, fix; coverage (states visited/skipped, flows reached/not reached); silently truncated exploration must never read as full coverage.
- *Safety:* never confirm destructive or real-money/real-comms actions.

**Key content — `visual-ux-review`.**
- *Mandatory driver conformance:* map six capabilities (navigate, non-destructive set-viewport, evaluate-js returning data, full-page PNG screenshot, accessibility snapshot, click); if any is missing, emit only `BROWSER_DRIVER_MISSING` and nothing else (no findings, scores, coordinates or verdict).
- *Scope from diff:* `gh pr diff --name-only` → changed route files → route; changed shared components → grep importing pages; confirm list with the user.
- *Capture at 320/375/768/1024/1440 px* (×900 height): full-page screenshot, interactive-element geometry (document-relative `getBoundingClientRect` + scroll), font-size audit (<16 px), horizontal overflow (`scrollWidth > clientWidth`), optional Lighthouse; a completion-gate grid must be filled for every breakpoint; skipped checks are reported as `NOT CHECKED`.
- *Mechanical checks:* targets < 24 px Critical, 24–43 px High, plus < 8 px gap to nearest interactive sibling; text < 12 px Critical, < 16 px High at mobile (labels/captions/legal may be 12–14); any overflow Critical on mobile/High on desktop; spacing deviation > 4 px from the group median = Medium; **layout stability: click every sort/filter/tab/pagination/expander, wait 500 ms, flag any neighbouring element moving > 5 px or resizing/disappearing**; alignment flag at ≥ 2 px (1 px tolerance).
- *Vision critique rubric* (9 dimensions with weights CRITICAL→LOW) applied adversarially (assume the UI is broken until shown otherwise).
- *Report & verdict:* every finding names element, breakpoint, evidence (a measurement such as `width=22px, threshold=44px`) and fix; **SHIP** = 0 Critical and 0 High; **SHIP WITH FIXES** = 0 Critical and 1–3 High; **BLOCK** = any Critical, ≥ 4 High, or a High with no clear fix.
- *Annotation integrity:* **coordinates are never estimated** — boxes must be the measured rect of that exact element at a single breakpoint; if no measured rect, omit the box ("a text-only finding beats a wrong box"); spot-check by re-querying before generating.
- *Human feedback loop:* `report.html` lets a reviewer mark each finding 👍 agree / 👎 disagree with a comment and export the votes to `ux-review-feedback.json` = `{reviews:[{page, id, title, severity, vote, comment}]}`, which the agent reads back to refine findings (`scripts/generate_report.py`, lines ~257–343).

**Weaknesses / cautions.** Single agent (no independent evaluators); severities are the author's (anything under 24 px is labelled a WCAG AA failure) and ignore 2.5.8's spacing, inline and essential exceptions (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum), so a measured sub-24 px target must be checked against the exceptions before it is called a WCAG failure; treating text under 16 px as High is an iOS-zoom heuristic, not a standard; the adversarial posture may inflate counts (no false-positive budget); Chrome DevTools MCP mapping explicitly unverified.

**Adopt.** Almost all of it, as our *live verification* layer: driver-conformance gate + hard stop; persona rules + most-obvious-action tie-break; per-step record schema; first-click rule; stuck-after-3 rule; state signature de-dup + budgets + coverage reporting; adversarial probe list; driver timeout ≠ finding and verify-persistence rules; measured-box-only annotations; NOT CHECKED reporting; SHIP/SHIP WITH FIXES/BLOCK rule; agree/disagree feedback export as the human-calibration channel.

- **What it is:** A Claude Code plugin (v0.6.0 per `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`) by Carl Sziebert with a persona/skill/command layering, a 1,080-line `SPEC.md`, a shared report contract with a Python validator, a CUJ (critical user journey) contract with a validator, a repo-footprint safety checker, an HTML renderer, a two-tier eval harness, and pytest suites with valid/invalid fixtures. 12 stars, last push 2026-08-25.
- **URL:** https://github.com/carlsz/ux-agent-skills
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Carl Sziebert) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** `SPEC.md` (§§1–9 in full, §10 up to the acceptance criteria; §11 auth-handoff only via the skill summaries), `skills/usability-audit/SKILL.md`, `skills/usability-audit/references/report-contract.md`, `ai-design-heuristics.md`, `npcis.md`, `skills/audit-cuj/SKILL.md` (384 lines), `agents/usability-auditor.md` (key rules), `evals/README.md`, `evals/cases/usability-audit.json`, `evals/cases/audit-cuj.json`, `evals/run_evals.py` (header + routing model), `scripts/validate_report.py` (head and rules), tree of `tests/fixtures/`.

**Key content — finding → evidence → `file:line`.**
- *Modes* (`skills/usability-audit/SKILL.md` step 1): auto-select **live** when a URL or running dev server exists, **static** (read source) otherwise, **hybrid** = live for runtime behaviour + static to locate the `file:line` where the fix belongs. Record the mode *actually* used (a requested live that fell back is reported as static, with the reason).
- *Render-vs-source honesty rule* (report contract §6): claims about runtime perception (status feedback, error recovery, latency, transitions) count as verified only when observed live; in static mode they must be labelled **`potential — unverified`** with the reason, because a false pass is worse than an honest gap.
- *De-duplicate and attribute* (step 4): the same defect surfacing under several frameworks (e.g., missing feedback = Nielsen #1 = Shneiderman #3 = NPCIS Interaction) becomes **one finding**, attributed to a primary framework with corroborating citations, so one defect is never filed several times.
- *Finding schema* (contract §5): heading `### [sevN] <title>` (N ∈ 1..4), with five mandatory labelled fields — **Issue Description, Framework Violation (primary + corroborating), Severity (0–4 + one-line justification), Evidence (screenshot under `./assets/`, `file:line`, and/or repro steps), Recommended Fix**. Severity 0 is a non-finding and must never appear.
- *Report frontmatter* (nine required keys): `auditor, schema, version, date (ISO-8601 UTC), target, mode, scope, frameworks, summary{total, sev4, sev3, sev2, sev1}`; body counts must reconcile with `summary` (validator-enforced); `version` must be read from `plugin.json` at run time (a live run once copied the example literal, and the contract treats that as an instruction bug); appendix must list **Coverage / not inspected** (silent gaps are prohibited) and an `Access:` line recording which auth step was used, never the account identity; append-only `index.md` with one row per run; stable screenshot names so re-runs overwrite assets but never reports.
- *Safety invariant* (`scripts/audit_safety.py`, SPEC §5.2): after a run, nothing changed outside `.ux/audits/` **and** the run's own writes inside it were observed (otherwise a run that wrote nothing would pass); content-addressed baseline snapshot taken *before* the run so the user's pre-existing dirty files are not blamed.
- *Auth handoff:* never enter credentials; observe the wall, ask once, the *user* signs in, re-observe, resume; never read cookies/localStorage/tokens; never leave scope while logged in.

**Key content — journey replay (`skills/audit-cuj/SKILL.md`, `skills/spec-cuj/references/cuj-contract.md`).**
- *CUJ file* (10 keys): `id` (`CUJ-\d{3}`), `schema`, `title`, `actor` (one specific sentence, not a generic user), `goal` (an outcome, not features), `criticality` (critical/high/medium/low), `entry_point`, `preconditions` (what must be TRUE, not steps), `steps[{n, action, expect}]`, `success_criteria` (evaluated after the steps). **`expect` carries the weight of the contract** — it must be observable (visible element, specific string, URL change, persisted value); vague outcomes (merely asserting that it works or saves) are rejected. Author/date/revision are deliberately excluded (git is the source of truth; also avoids PII).
- *Authoring persona refusals:* reject generic actors, unobservable outcomes, feature-list goals, bundled steps, criticality inflation; never invent a step. Interview rule: the agent may *guess* descriptive fields but must *elicit* the graded fields (`expect`, `success_criteria`, `criticality`) openly, refusing a bare yes.
- *Replay:* snapshot repo first; **zero journeys selected = stop, never a pass**; validate journeys and paste real validator output; never repair a journey during an audit; precondition ladder **L0 observe (record baseline verbatim) → L1 replay a setup journey (only counts if it passed its own success criteria) → L2 improvised UI setup (re-observe after reload) → L2.5 named reset (ask first) → L3 ask the user**; hard stop on fixtures/seed scripts/DB writes/`evaluate_script` state injection (state injected that way is unreachable for real users, so it proves nothing); **no-alibi rule** — a self-referential precondition is never a skip; every skip names a cause and a `Repair:` line. Steps replayed in order with verbatim `expect`, observed result and evidence; stop at first failing step, later steps are reported as not observed, never as passes. `success_criteria` checked separately after a reload; **an absence criterion needs a non-empty baseline** (otherwise a broken app passes vacuously).
- *Severity:* two stages — classify (4 journey blocked; 3 workaround required *or* steps pass but success criteria fail, e.g., silent data loss; 2 observable mismatch but goal met; 1 cosmetic; 0 match → omitted) then **clamp by the journey's criticality** (critical/high uncapped, medium max 3, low max 2). Cascades: a journey blocked by another's failure is reported as skipped with a pointer to the blocking step and finding, and the blocker gets an `Also blocks:` line — "One bug, one finding."
- *Honest denominators:* summary leads with the line `P/N journeys passed, S skipped, M of T steps verified`, where N = journeys *selected*, never journeys run; `frameworks` claims only what was proved (`task-completion` only if the journey was actually run live; static runs emit `cuj-contract` alone).

**Key content — evals for the skill itself (`evals/`).** Tier 2 (deterministic, CI): every skill needs ≥3 positive trigger prompts (must rank within `top_k`), ≥2 negative prompts owned by a named competitor skill (must not rank first), ≥1 behavioural eval; routing approximated by stemmed TF-IDF over skill descriptions; description-collision error at cosine ≥0.75, warning ≥0.50; reports rank-1 rate. Tier 3 (behavioural, on demand): run against a real app (Sprout), then `--grade` validates reports, runs the safety check, auto-checks mechanical expectations and flags semantic ones as `trust_level: provisional` for LLM/human grading. Crucially, eval `audit-cuj #2` **deliberately breaks** the add-task input and requires a sev-4 finding naming the exact step: a verifier that only ever passes is exactly what the eval is designed to catch.

**Strengths.** The most rigorous engineering of any repo surveyed: machine-checkable contracts, executable honesty rules, vacuous-pass defences, footprint verification, seeded-defect behavioural evals, and append-only longitudinal index.

**Weaknesses.** Single evaluator; no multi-evaluator merge; severity is assigned by the auditor (no post-hoc rating); NPCIS is a proprietary consultancy framework (YUJ Designs, https://www.yujdesigns.com/blog/heuristic-evaluation-ux-design/), not a research standard; no feedback intake from real users; heavy process for small UIs.

**Adopt.** Finding schema with mandatory five fields + `file:line`; render-vs-source honesty labels; one-defect-one-finding with primary/corroborating citations; `summary` reconciliation; Coverage/not-inspected appendix; append-only run index; footprint check with pre-run baseline; CUJ contract (observable `expect`, preconditions ≠ steps, success criteria after reload); precondition ladder + no-alibi + no state injection; two-stage severity with criticality clamp; cascade → `Also blocks:`; honest denominators; trigger/behavioural evals with a seeded-defect case.

- **What it is:** Two Claude Code sub-agents and two portable prompts that turn raw usability-test (UT) transcripts into analyses: a fast 6-tag sprint analyzer (`ut-transcript-analyzer`) and a 14-stage deep analyzer with an 8-stage cross-user synthesis (`ut-research-analyzer`, 1,143 lines). 2 stars, last push 2026-03-08.

- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Soyeon Lee) — adaptable with attribution.

**Key content.**
- *Evidence rules* (`agents/ut-research-analyzer.md`, Core Principles): verbatim first (quote, don't summarise); no speculation — mark *No evidence* when nothing supports a claim; **timestamps required** on every quote; **interviewer-influence tracking** — every coded utterance has a `Prompted?` flag (prompted vs spontaneous), prompted utterances are not weighted equally and the prompted ratio lowers confidence; no subjective language (impressions of interest or confusion are banned — describe behaviour instead); interviewer speaking ratio is computed to spot over-talking moderators; STT errors annotated `[original -> estimated]` only when they change meaning.

The copy-vs-structural split (can copy/labels fix it, or does the feature need redesign?) mirrors the fast analyser's UX-issue-vs-structural-issue rule.

- *Cross-user synthesis (C1–C8):* side-by-side verbatim per feature; reaction classes **common positive/negative = 2+ users**, split reactions (check segment differences), unique reactions; hypothesis cross-validation (validated when at least two thirds of users support it) with a confidence column adjusted by prompted-utterance ratio; theme cross-mapping, persona consolidation, then Olsen's importance–satisfaction gap, PMF pyramid and Kano.
- *Design rationale* (`docs/design-rationale.md`): explains why 14 stages (Braun & Clarke thematic analysis, Norman's gulfs of execution/evaluation, 7 stages of action, Peak-End), and explicitly excludes cognitive walkthrough (it needs no user data) and A/B interpretation.

**Strengths.** Strongest *evidence-provenance* rules for qualitative user data in this survey (verbatim + timestamp + prompted flag + utterance IDs referenced by issues); clear separation of copy fixes vs structural redesign; honest not-validated/ambiguous verdicts.

**Weaknesses.** Severity (1–4, no 0) assigned by one LLM pass from one transcript — no frequency across participants until the cross-user stage; 14 stages are heavy and partly framework-stacking; Nielsen tagging of empirical issues is post-hoc and can be forced; the two-thirds-of-users threshold is ad hoc for typical n = 5–8; no link to code or to the UI under test.

**Adopt.** Verbatim + timestamp + `Prompted?` flag as mandatory fields of any user-feedback evidence item; issue rows cite evidence IDs; copy-vs-structural fix type; cross-participant frequency (n of N participants) as the frequency input to severity; explicit ambiguous/not-validated outcome.

### 2.10 AndersonWang/heuristics-evaluation-skill (frequency × impact × persistence → 0–4)

- **What it is:** A small Claude Code plugin (one skill, four progressive-disclosure references) for Nielsen heuristic evaluation with a three-factor severity calculation matrix and a cognitive-walkthrough reference. 1 star, last push 2026-04-01.
- **URL:** https://github.com/AndersonWang/heuristics-evaluation-skill
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Anderson Wang) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** `plugins/heuristics-evaluation/skills/heuristics-evaluation/SKILL.md`, `references/severity-guide.md`, `references/cognitive-walkthrough.md`, `references/report-template.md`, `references/heuristics-reference.md` (head), `README.md`.

**Key content.**
- *Process:* plan scope → **first pass (explore freely), second pass (systematic per heuristic)** → log each issue with location + severity + recommendation → report. It is the only single-agent skill here that encodes Nielsen's two-pass rule.
- *Principles:* quote the UI (name the exact label); keep heuristics separate from taste; **"One finding per card"** — never bundle issues even when they share a heuristic (Nielsen's list-each-problem-separately rule); acknowledge strengths; calibrate to context (severity 3 on an internal tool may be 4 on consumer checkout).
- *Output:* summary table `Severity | Heuristic | Location | Issue` sorted by severity; per-finding cards for severity ≥ 2 (`Where`, `Observation`, `Why it's a problem`, `Recommendation`); closing note with the single highest-leverage fix; formal report template with counts per severity and top 3 actions.
- *Severity guide* (`references/severity-guide.md`): factors Frequency (Rare/Occasional/Frequent), Impact (how badly it affects task completion; Low/Medium/High), Persistence (whether users can work around it or recover easily; Easy/Moderate/Difficult), a 12-row lookup offered as guidance rather than a formula, context modifiers (consumer vs internal; first-time vs returning; **+1 if a mistake costs data, money or a support contact**; homepage ≈ 100% of users vs settings < 1%), and one-line shorthand tests per level (4: users fail or abandon; 3: users are frustrated and slowed but push through; 2: users are annoyed but work around it; 1: users probably will not notice; 0: on reflection not a problem).
- *CW reference:* Wharton four questions answered Yes / **Maybe** / No; any No or Maybe is logged; summary of overall success likelihood as High/Medium/Low (qualitative, not a fabricated percentage).

**Errors found (checked locally by enumerating all 27 factor combinations).**
1. The lookup table leaves **8 of 27 combinations uncovered** (e.g., Rare/High/Easy, Occasional/High/Easy, Frequent/Medium/Easy, Occasional/Low/Difficult) and has **one conflict** (Frequent/Low/Easy matches both the rule Frequent·Low·Any → 2 and the rule Any·Low·Easy → 1).
2. **Factor definitions drift from Nielsen:** Nielsen's *impact* asks how easy or difficult the problem is for users to overcome, and *persistence* asks whether it is a one-off that users get past once they know it or something that keeps bothering them (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/). This skill's persistence factor (can users work around it) is Nielsen's *impact*, and Nielsen's persistence (recurrence) is missing. By contrast, the uw-ssec matrix (§2.3) uses Nielsen's definitions (impact = ease of overcoming, persistence = one-time vs recurring) and covers all 18 cells of its 3 × 2 × 3 grid, monotonically.

**Adopt.** Two-pass procedure; one finding per card; quote the UI; context modifiers (especially +1 when an error costs data, money or a support contact); qualitative CW summary instead of percentages; shorthand tests as rater anchors. **Do not adopt** the lookup table as-is; build our own complete, monotone table using Nielsen's factor definitions and verify coverage with a unit test.

### 2.11 ogatakatsuya/UXCascade (unofficial implementation of UXCascade, UIST 2026)

- **What it is:** An *unofficial* FastAPI + React re-implementation of **UXCascade: Scalable Usability Testing with Simulated User Agents** (Holter, Koh, Dogan, Chan; arXiv:2601.15777, accepted at UIST 2026). Persona-grounded browser agents (browser-use) run goals on a live site; post-hoc agents tag reasoning and extract issues; an editor agent produces DOM patches; a preview agent re-simulates one step to test the fix. 10 stars, last push 2026-02-26.
- **URLs:** https://github.com/ogatakatsuya/UXCascade ; paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15777 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15777)
- **License:** MIT (copyright holder: UXCascade Contributors); the prompts are labelled as reproductions of the paper's Appendix C, so the *paper's* copyright also applies to the prompt text — **reuse ideas, rewrite prompts**.
- **Files read:** `README.md`; `backend/app/agents/issue_detector_agent.py`, `tagging_agent.py`, `preview_agent.py`, `simulation_agent.py` (prompts); `domain/services/persona_matrix.py`, `goal_aggregator.py`; `domain/value_objects/persona.py`, `enums.py`. Paper HTML read via WebFetch (study design and limitations).

**Key content.**
- *Persona matrix* (`domain/services/persona_matrix.py`): Cartesian product of trait dimensions (paper default: price sensitivity, time pressure, age cohort, user type — four binary traits) × **2 agents per combination** = 32 agents, for more behavioural diversity.
- *Simulation prompt* (`agents/simulation_agent.py`): first-person think-aloud as the persona; for each element state what I see / what I expect / how it aligns with my goals; list persona-appropriate questions; return to the site if a link leaves it.
- *Tagging agent:* ≤3 cognitive-intent tags per step (explore/browse, search/locate, select/decide, input/submit, confirm/complete, adjust/undo, learn/understand, troubleshoot) — tags describe intent rather than low-level UI actions; temperature 0; post-hoc.
- *Issue detector* (`agents/issue_detector_agent.py`): per simulation step, every distinct issue with fields **`type`, `element`, `reason`, `fix`, `upt_codes`, `upt_explanation`, `issue_severity` (Nielsen 0–4)**; steps without issues return `[]`; strict JSON with exactly N steps (code pads/trims mismatches). UPT codes are a condensed 19-code version (A Visualness, B Language, C Manipulation, D Task-Mapping, E Task-Facilitation) of Keenan, Hartson et al.'s Usability Problem Taxonomy, whose original has 28 categories (19 artifact, 9 task) built from 400+ real problem reports (Empirical Software Engineering 4:71–104, 1999; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009855231530).
- *Aggregation* (`goal_aggregator.py`): per goal — agent count, success rate, all issues, and **trait distributions** (which trait values encountered issues most).
- *Fix evaluation with sycophancy mitigation* (`agents/preview_agent.py`): rather than asking an LLM whether the fix is good, re-run the **same step with the same prompt history on the patched HTML** and compare the next action; `issue_resolved = action_changed`; the LLM summary is only supplementary.
- *Paper's evaluation* (arXiv HTML): within-subjects study with **8 UX practitioners** (6.5 ± 4 years), 7-min exploration then 10 min per condition (human evaluation report baseline vs UXCascade, counterbalanced), measures = number of **seeded usability issues** found, NASA-TLX, interviews. Limitations reported: agents show lower behavioural variability than humans; thin trait-based personas; difficulty with subtle visual cues; single-step re-simulation misses downstream ripple effects; some participants accepted agent output at face value while others doubted its realism. No systematic false-positive measurement is reported.

**Strengths.** Clean decomposition (simulate → tag → detect → patch → re-simulate); per-step issue schema with taxonomy codes and severity; trait-level aggregation; behavioural (action-change) rather than self-reported fix verification.

**Weaknesses.** Agents are not real users (the paper says so); no false-positive gate; `action_changed` is a weak proxy (a changed action is not necessarily a better one); unofficial code; patches are DOM patches on snapshots, not source changes.

**Adopt.** Issue schema fields (`type`, `element`, `reason`, `fix`, taxonomy code + explanation, severity); persona trait matrix with ≥2 agents per cell when we simulate; per-trait distribution of issues; **behavioural fix verification** — re-run the failing step/journey after the fix and require an observable change in the outcome (and, stronger than UXCascade, that the goal is now reached), never by asking a model whether the fix is good.

### 2.12 Jackwwj619/UXBench (benchmark: actionability of LLM-generated UX critiques)

- **What it is:** Code, fixtures and run artefacts for **UXBench: Measuring the Actionability of LLM-Generated UX Critiques** (Wang, Huang, … Zhang; University of Notre Dame corresponding author; arXiv:2606.16262, cs.SE). A judge model explores a runnable web fixture in Playwright under a coverage gate and writes a structured critique; a **fixed** repair agent (Claude Code) edits the fixture from the critique; a **fixed** scorer (GPT-5.4-Mini, blinded to judge identity) rates the repaired page; the judge's quality is Δ = score(repaired) − score(original). 4 stars, last push 2026-07-31.
- **URLs:** https://github.com/Jackwwj619/UXBench ; paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16262 ; project page https://jackwwj619.github.io/projects/UXBench/
- **License:** **No LICENSE file in the repository** (GitHub reports none) although the README's last section states an MIT release. Legally that leaves the code all-rights-reserved until a license file is added — **treat as ideas only; ask the authors before reusing code or fixtures**.
- **Files read:** `README.md`; `uxagent/schemas.py` (all Pydantic models), `uxagent/agent.py` (coverage gate: constants and `build_coverage_state`), `uxagent/judge_brain.py` (rubric text, scale anchors, `build_metric_score_caps`), `uxagent/claude_code.py` (repair prompt), `Agent-run-results/results-gpt-5.4/booking/feedback.json`; paper PDF (downloaded, text-extracted; §3.1–4.3, Table 1–2, limitations).

**Benchmark design.**
- *Fixtures:* **41 local-first runnable fixtures in 10 surface families** (landing, pricing, onboarding, booking, dashboard, docs, privacy/settings, data-viz, chatbot/agent, mobile UI): **11 real-product anchors** (e.g., notion, slack pricing, shopify & GOV.UK passport onboarding, booking, cloudflare-radar, stripe-docs, microsoft-privacy, OWID, chatgpt) + **30 independently authored synthetic siblings** that keep the interaction model but change branding, copy, layout and visual identity — anchors give external validity, siblings test that the judge evaluates the page in front of it "rather than memorized impressions of well-known products" (paper §3.1).
- *Coverage-gated exploration* (`uxagent/agent.py`, paper Algorithm 1): minimum steps by depth quick/standard/deep/exhaustive = 6/12/20/32; minimum unique pages 4/6/8/10 or a share (0.25/0.40/0.65/1.00) of known pages; minimum *feature* coverage 0/35/55/70% of visible controls exercised; in both-viewport mode at least max(8, min(16, 20% of budget)) mobile steps; plan phases must include interaction, not just page opens. If the judge tries to finish early, the environment rejects it with the unmet reasons, retries, then takes a deterministic fallback action (open an unvisited page or exercise an unexplored control).
- *Evidence-tied reporting:* a finding is valid only if linked to an observed interaction event. Report schema `FeedbackIssue{id, severity: high|medium|low, area, page_hint, problem, evidence, suggested_fix}` plus `intent_completed` (`uxagent/schemas.py`). The example `feedback.json` cites measured evidence (content width in px against a 390 px viewport).
- *Scoring with evidence caps* (`uxagent/judge_brain.py`): seven metrics (goal-state clarity, navigation scent, action feedback, flow efficiency, error recovery, trust/consequence transparency, scanability & responsive accessibility). Anchors: 9–10 excellent with direct positive evidence … 1–2 critical. Rules: **high scores require direct positive evidence — the mere absence of visible problems is not enough**; **without direct positive evidence the score stays at 6 or below**; deterministic caps when evidence is missing — e.g., no state-changing action observed → action feedback ≤ 5; no error/recovery path exercised → error recovery ≤ 5; mobile never exercised → scanability ≤ 6; fewer than 2 pages → navigation/flow ≤ 6. Each metric records `positive_evidence`, `negative_evidence`, `missing_evidence`, `score_cap_reason`, confidence. (The paper reports a 1–5 ordinal scale per metric, equally weighted; the code's judge prompt uses 1–10 — the mapping between them is **[unverified]**.)
- *Repair invariants* (`uxagent/claude_code.py`): fresh copy of baseline per run; only the working copy may be edited; preserve scenario, content intent and visual language; smallest cohesive change set; skip and note what cannot be fixed safely.
- *Results* (paper Table 1, README): all eight judges start at the same baseline 3.27/5 and lift it by **+0.14 (Gemini-3.1-Pro) to +0.22 (GPT-5.4)**, all significant per fixture (paired t / Wilcoxon, p < 0.05); per-dimension lifts are largest for action feedback (+0.24…+0.44) and smallest for flow efficiency (+0.03…+0.12); no judge dominates all ten families; **blind human review by 6 participants with UX/front-end experience** broadly agrees but compresses the top into a GPT-5.4 / Claude-Sonnet-4.6 cluster.

**Strengths.** The only benchmark here that measures *usefulness of critiques for repair* rather than their resemblance to human text; anchor/sibling design against memorisation; coverage gate and evidence caps are directly reusable false-high controls; pairwise win-rates instead of means alone.

**Weaknesses (own analysis).** Lifts are small in absolute terms (≤ 0.22 on 1–5); the fixed scorer (GPT-5.4-Mini) is also one of the eight judges, a potential self-preference confound that I did not see discussed [verify in appendix]; static fixtures cannot show backend failures; human study is small (n = 6); repair by a single agent couples actionability to that agent's skills.

**Adopt.** As the backbone of **our own skill's benchmark**: (1) runnable fixtures with real anchors + re-skinned siblings, (2) coverage-gated exploration with explicit stop conditions, (3) evidence-capped scoring (no positive evidence → at most 6/10), (4) Δ after a fixed repair agent as the actionability metric, (5) blind human spot-review of repaired outputs, (6) pairwise win-rates per surface family. For our audit outputs: every issue needs `evidence` from an observed interaction and a `page_hint`/location.

### 2.13 noodisD/UXAgent (production fork of UXAgent with false-positive verification gates)

- **What it is:** A Flask + Playwright + Vue platform (by robertkrivic) derived from neuhai/UXAgent, built to audit Polish e-commerce sites with synthetic personas; its distinctive contribution is a chain of **deterministic and LLM gates that verify each finding against the interaction trace** before it reaches a report, plus 281 backend tests. 0 stars, last push 2026-09-01.
- **URL:** https://github.com/noodisD/UXAgent
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 robertkrivic) for the new audit/report pipeline; `NOTICE` states the agent loop, browser executor and memory system come from neuhai/UXAgent under MIT and the notice must be retained. Adaptable with attribution to both.
- **Files read:** `README.md`, `NOTICE`, `METHODOLOGY_PLAN.md` (phases 1–6), `backend/src/simulated_web_agent/audit_prompts/verify_findings.txt`, `heuristic_evaluate.txt`, `cognitive_walkthrough.txt`; `main/claim_verifier.py`, `attribution.py` (head), `evidence.py`, `severity.py`, `confidence.py`, `root_cause.py`, `prioritization.py` (design notes), `site_scope.py` (design notes).

**The ground truth that motivated it** (README, `claim_verifier.py` docstring): a site team's manual check of a 5-persona report judged only **~2 of 8 findings real**; most false ones were the agent's own interaction difficulties reported as site defects, or the agent not noticing an element that was present. On the largest run (20 personas × 70 steps) the gates cut **98 raw findings to 28**; agent self-rated task success disagreed with trace-grounded success **14 times in 20 personas**. These are the developer's own measurements on their runs (not peer-reviewed) but they are the only quantified false-positive data in this survey.

**The gate chain (all deterministic unless noted).**
1. *Evidence resolution* (`main/evidence.py`): the LLM only *cites* a step; the code checks the step exists, **replaces the claimed URL with the URL the trace recorded** (keeping `claimed_url` if different), attaches the step screenshot and a 400-char observation quote, and stamps `verified`. Findings without a real step citation are discarded as unverifiable (prompt rule in `heuristic_evaluate.txt`).
2. *Harness attribution* (`main/attribution.py`): findings whose cited step is a **harness interaction failure** are flagged `harness_suspect` — signatures: a harness fallback action (recorded when an element failed after several attempts), the same (action, target) repeated **3 times consecutively** (session-wide counting was rejected because it flagged legitimate repeated searches), and **perception artifacts** (observation text ≤ 80 chars while raw HTML ≥ 20,000 bytes — empty-page claims that were really overlay perception failures).
3. *Site scoping* (`main/site_scope.py`): findings whose trace-resolved URL is outside the target's registrable domain are `off_site` (an agent once wandered to a competitor and filed 6 findings about it).
4. *Claim verification* (`main/claim_verifier.py` + `audit_prompts/verify_findings.txt`, LLM): a skeptical-auditor prompt re-reads the **full observation** (≤ 4,000 chars) of the cited step plus the actions before/at/after, and session facts proved by the trace (e.g., product pages opened, cart reached). Verdicts: **supported / partial / unsupported**. Hard rules: agent difficulty is not evidence of a site defect; **absence claims are supported only if the observation shows the region where the element belongs and the element is not in it**; a claimed mechanism contradicted by the observed state is unsupported; conventional behaviour (login at guest checkout, button disabled until a required option) is at most partial; judge only from given evidence; a claim supported only by the agent's own narrated reflection is unsupported. **Deterministic override:** if the session itself refutes a broken/absent claim (e.g., ≥ 2 product pages were opened, so a claim that product links do not work is false; or the observation contains the cart indicator claimed missing), force `unsupported` even if the LLM said supported. **Fail-open**: if the verifier errors, findings stay as they were ("An unverified report is honest; a silently emptied one is not").
5. *Exclusion and severity* (`main/severity.py`): one predicate `excluded_from_report` = harness_suspect OR off_site OR claim_verdict == unsupported; excluded findings are rendered separately as needing verification and scored **severity 0 = false positive** (NN/g 0 reinterpreted); otherwise LLM labels map krytyczny 4 / poważny 3 / drobny 2.
6. *Confidence ceiling* (`main/confidence.py`): self-reported confidence was useless (56 of 59 findings self-rated high and low was never used), so **evidence sets the ceiling and the model may only lower it**: harness_suspect/off_site/unsupported/unverified → low; partial → medium; else high; record `confidence_capped_by`.
7. *Root-cause clustering* (`main/root_cause.py`): one bug (stale search results) had been filed **six times under six different heuristics** by six personas; the fix is a small library of **named, evidence-grounded signatures** requiring both a context marker and a symptom marker — deliberately *not* embedding similarity, to avoid merging genuinely different bugs (precision over recall).
8. *Prioritization* (`main/prioritization.py`): impact is **derived, not asked** — reach (personas reporting it; widespread = ≥ 50% of panel **and** ≥ 2 personas) × consequence (any reporting persona failed/abandoned per the trace-observed outcome); high = widespread AND blocking, medium = either, low = neither; every row ships `personas_affected`, `participant_count`, `blocked_personas` so readers can apply their own rule; the module states its known weakness (a persona that failed for another reason marks all its findings as blocking).

**Methodology rules worth copying** (`METHODOLOGY_PLAN.md`): AI agents serve pre-testing and hypothesis generation, not as a representative user sample; ISO 9241-11 split of effectiveness / efficiency / satisfaction, with synthetic satisfaction **always labelled `synthetic_self_report`**; forbid population phrasing such as k-of-k participants — write it as a synthetic hypothesis observed in N of M simulated personas; finding schema adds `severity_ai_proposed`, `severity_human_confirmed`, `requires_real_user_validation`, `confidence`; **separate task scenario from harness hints** (scenarios must not reveal how to use the UI; lint for strings naming UI controls); machine-checkable `success_condition` (URL pattern / element present / state reached); WCAG checks scoped honestly — 24×24 is normative, 48×48 is advisory; with the 2.5.8 spacing exception one site's 27 reported tap-target failures became 4; contrast over images/gradients is `undetermined`, never scored; 2.5.7 drag alternatives need human check; planned validation module computing **precision/recall of problem discovery vs 5–8 real users, Cohen's κ for task success, Spearman for severity rank**, with a pilot target of > 70% recall of critical problems.

**Weaknesses.** Marker lists are Polish- and e-commerce-specific; root-cause library has one signature; validation module is planned, not built; synthetic agents remain synthetic.

**Adopt (high priority).** The whole gate chain as a pattern: cite-a-step → trace-resolved evidence → harness/agent-failure filter → scope filter → skeptical claim verifier with absence rule and deterministic overrides → exclusion = severity 0 shown separately → evidence-capped confidence → named-signature root-cause merge → derived impact. Also: never trust model self-ratings (confidence, impact, success); derive them from evidence.

### 2.15 AIG-ist-tugraz/MLLM-Usability-Improvements (FSE 2026 replication package)

- **What it is:** Replication package for *Recommending Usability Improvements with Multimodal Large Language Models* (Lubos, Felfernig, Garber, Le, Henrich — Graz University of Technology; FSE 2026; arXiv:2604.25420; Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19498008). Input = app description + persona + task + **screen recording of a user doing the task**; an MLLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash, temperature 0) evaluates each Nielsen heuristic separately; findings are clustered, summarised, re-ranked; software engineers rated the top recommendations. 2 stars, last push 2026-05-18.
- **URL:** https://github.com/AIG-ist-tugraz/MLLM-Usability-Improvements
- **License:** MIT (year 2026, holder unnamed in `LICENSE`; `CITATION.cff` lists the authors). Adaptable with attribution/citation.
- **Files read:** `README.md`, `evaluation/LLM_Usability_Evaluation.ipynb` (all prompt and aggregation cells), `user_study/survey/survey_structure.md`, `user_study/responses/*.csv` (12 files, parsed), `data/*/task_description.json` structure, `CITATION.cff`.

**Pipeline (notebook cells 11–27).**
1. *Per-heuristic, per-task evaluation:* for every persona × task × heuristic, send the video + app description + persona + task + one heuristic with its explanation; the system prompt asks for each heuristic to be assessed independently and strictly from the provided materials, without assumptions; output `{heuristic, issues:[{description, recommendation, severity: No Issue | Minor Issue | Major Issue}]}`, 1–5 sentences each.
2. *Dedup by semantic similarity:* within a task, embed descriptions with `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` and greedily cluster pairs with cosine ≥ **0.70**; each cluster keeps all heuristics; descriptions and recommendations of multi-item clusters are summarised by the LLM.
3. *Severity of a cluster:* **majority vote; ties resolve to Major Issue** (conservative).
4. *Re-ranking:* a second prompt re-ranks clusters by actual severity, considering impact on task success and frustration, frequency/likelihood, and effort to diagnose and recover; preliminary severity and heuristic labels are **removed before ranking so they cannot bias the ranker**; output includes a justification per item.
5. *Human evaluation* (`survey_structure.md`): participants watch the recording, note issues themselves first, then rate each of three top recommendations: issue clearly formulated (1–5), issue plausible for this recording/task (1–5), recommendation clear (1–5), recommendation plausible (1–5), completeness (Complete / Partially / Incomplete + what is missing), and "**Would you have detected this issue on your own?**".

**Numbers I computed from the shipped responses** (95 responses × 3 recommendations = 285 ratings across both apps; not taken from the paper): mean issue clarity 4.21/5, issue plausibility 4.04/5, recommendation clarity 4.28/5, recommendation plausibility 4.10/5; completeness 210 complete / 65 partial / 10 incomplete; would-have-detected-it-myself answers Yes 137 / No 94 / Don't know 54 — i.e., roughly a third of top-ranked issues were ones the rater says they would have missed. The paper's own figures and participant count should be checked in the PDF **[not verified against the paper text]**.

**Strengths.** Uses *real user interaction recordings* as evidence (closest to empirical data among the LLM pipelines here); one-heuristic-per-call design forces coverage of all ten; explicit dedup and tie-breaking rules; bias-avoiding re-rank (strip prior labels); a reusable human-rating instrument (is the issue plausible for this recording, is the fix complete, would the rater have found it alone).

**Weaknesses.** Three-level severity (no 0–4); embedding clustering can merge different bugs (the noodisD analysis argues for named signatures instead); no evidence timestamps tying an issue to a video moment; the evaluation measures perceived plausibility, not whether fixes improved usability.

**Adopt.** Per-heuristic independent passes as one of our "evaluator" variants; dedup clusters keep *all* heuristic labels; majority-vote severity with conservative tie-break; strip prior severity/labels before an independent re-rating pass (mirrors Nielsen's advice to rate severity afterwards and independently); the human rating instrument (clarity, plausibility for the recording, completeness, would-I-have-found-it) for validating our skill's findings; require a **timestamp** when evidence is a video.

### 2.16 ruxailab/RUXAILAB + ruxailab/ai-heuristic-evaluation

- **What they are:** RUXAILAB (Remote User eXperience Artificial Intelligence LAB, by Uramaki LAB) is an open-source Vue 3 + Firebase platform for running heuristic evaluations, user tests (tasks, NASA-TLX, SART, TAM, facial sentiment), card sorting, focus groups and accessibility checks with remote participants; 183 stars, very active (push 2026-10-01). `ai-heuristic-evaluation` is a FastAPI service combining Microsoft OmniParser element detection with an LLM to score screenshots per Nielsen heuristic; 6 stars, last push 2026-01-02.
- **URLs:** https://github.com/ruxailab/RUXAILAB ; https://github.com/ruxailab/ai-heuristic-evaluation
- **Licenses:** RUXAILAB — MIT (© 2021 Uramaki LAB). ai-heuristic-evaluation — **no LICENSE file** (GitHub: none) → ideas only.
- **Files read:** RUXAILAB `src/ux/Heuristic/utils/statistics.js`, `models/HeuristicQuestionAnswer.js`, `ai-agents/README.md`, `weight_function/README.md` (AHP), heuristic module file list. ai-heuristic-evaluation `README.md`, `app/services/heuristic_engine.py` (full prompt + scoring), `app/core/constants.py` (criteria + weights), `tests/ground_truth.json`, `tests/test_integration_real.py`.

**RUXAILAB — multi-evaluator heuristic evaluation as a data model.**
- Each heuristic has questions; each evaluator answers with a configured option (numeric `value`), may mark **N/A** (excluded from the denominator) or a **warning** (uncertain answer), and may attach multiple comments and images per question (`HeuristicQuestionAnswer`: `heuristicId`, `heuristicAnswer{text, value}`, `comments[{id, text, createdAt, updatedAt}]`, `images[{id, url, createdAt}]`).
- Per evaluator, `calcFinalResult` gives result = Σ values / (applicable questions × max option) as a percentage, plus a **bounded range for warnings**: base (warnings excluded from numerator), max (warnings counted at the best option) and min (counted at the worst option) — an honest way to show how uncertain answers could move the score.
- Across evaluators, `finalResult` reports **average, max, min and standard deviation** of evaluator results, plus total comments/images and time spent.
- **AI agents as separate evaluators** (`src/ux/Heuristic/ai-agents/README.md`): an LLM agent's answer is stored with a `userDocId` prefixed `ai-agent:` "so it remains a separate evaluator in aggregate analytics"; the agent may request an element screenshot as visual proof; screenshot failure does not discard the text evaluation; provider keys stay server-side.
- *AHP weights* (`weight_function/README.md`): optional Saaty pairwise comparisons to weight heuristics. **Error:** the README's description of Saaty's fundamental scale lists 1–5 as equal…extreme and treats 6–9 as inverse values; Saaty's actual scale is 1 (equal), 3 (moderate), 5 (strong), 7 (very strong), 9 (extreme) with 2/4/6/8 intermediate and reciprocals for the inverse comparison (Saaty 1980; e.g., https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_hierarchy_process) **[secondary source — check the original]**.

**ai-heuristic-evaluation — screenshot → elements → per-heuristic LLM verdicts.**
- OmniParser detects elements (type, bbox, interactivity, content); the engine serialises *only* real detector fields, explicitly to avoid relying on attributes the detector never produces (such as hover state) and asks the LLM, per heuristic, for violations of **measurable criteria** (e.g., H1.1 loading states visible, H1.2 feedback for actions, H1.3 progress indicators; H3.3 confirmation for destructive actions) with `criterion_id`, `severity` (critical/major/minor/cosmetic), `description`, `affected_elements`, `recommendation`; RAG examples must be cited with a reference tag.
- Score per heuristic = 100 − Σ criterion-specific severity weights (e.g., H1.1 critical 10, major 5, minor 2, cosmetic 1).
- The test ground truth covers only element detection (a sign-in button and an input on a wireframe); the evaluation output itself is untested.
- **Problem (own analysis):** many criteria (loading states, feedback after clicks, confirmation dialogs) are *dynamic* and cannot be judged from one static screenshot's element list, so the LLM is invited to infer them — exactly the static-evidence over-reach that EliaAlberti's not-assessable list and carlsz's `potential — unverified` label exist to prevent.

**Adopt.** Treat AI evaluators as *labelled members of a panel* (never merged invisibly with humans); per-question N/A and uncertain states with min/max bounds; mean ± SD and range across evaluators; per-criterion IDs under each heuristic (H1.1, H1.2 …) so findings are comparable. **Do not adopt** single-screenshot judgement of dynamic criteria, or 100-minus-weights scores as a quality grade.

### 2.17 google-research-datasets/uicrit (UICrit dataset, UIST '24)

- **What it is:** A CSV of human design critiques with normalised bounding boxes and quality ratings for 1,000 RICO mobile screens, from Duan, Chen, Li, Hartmann & Li, *UICrit: Enhancing Automated Design Evaluation with a UI Critique Dataset* (UIST '24, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3654777.3676381; arXiv:2407.08850). 28 stars; static since 2024-11.
- **URL:** https://github.com/google-research-datasets/uicrit
- **License:** **CC BY 4.0** (README) — data reusable (including commercially) **with attribution**; GitHub shows no LICENSE file, the README statement governs.
- **Files read:** `README.md`; `uicrit_public.csv` parsed in full with Python (2,981 rows).

**Content.** Columns: `rico_id`, `task` (what the screen is for), `aesthetics_rating` (1–10), `learnability` (1–5), `efficency` [sic] (1–5), `usability_rating` (1–10), `design_quality_rating` (1–10), `comments_source` (human, llm — Gemini — or both), `comments` — each comment follows a fixed three-part template "**The expected standard is … In the current design … To fix this …**" plus `Bounding Box: [x1, y1, x2, y2]` normalised to the screenshot. The public version has ~3 annotators per screen (966 screens × 3; 11,344 critiques: 8,393 human-only, 1,893 "both", 1,058 LLM-only, my count), three times the paper's single-annotator set (paper: 3,059 critiques for 983 UIs from seven experienced designers; few-shot + visual prompting improved LLM-generated feedback by 55% on the paper's measure, still below human evaluators — arXiv abstract).

**Finding from my own analysis (important for how we score).** On screens with three annotators, holistic ratings show **essentially no inter-rater agreement**: one-way ICC(1) = 0.05 (usability), 0.03 (design quality), 0.02 (aesthetics); ICC for the mean of three raters = 0.13 / 0.07 / 0.07; Pearson correlation between the first two annotators across 999 screens ≈ −0.03 / −0.05 / −0.01. Mean within-screen SD ≈ 0.7 on the 1–10 scales while the between-screen variance is small (usability 0.41 vs total 1.13). Caveat: different annotators may use different anchors, and the paper's analysis used one annotator per screen; but the result strongly suggests that **holistic 1–10 quality scores (from humans or LLMs) are not a reliable ground truth**, whereas localised, templated critiques are usable.

**Strengths.** Expert, localised critiques with a reusable *standard → current → fix* template and bounding boxes; source labelling of human vs LLM vs both; permissive license.

**Weaknesses.** Static mobile screenshots (2010s RICO apps), no interaction; no severity field; holistic ratings unreliable (above); no heuristic tags.

**Adopt.** The three-part critique template (expected standard → what the current design does → how to fix) as the *description* format of our findings; normalised bbox field; source label (human / agent / both) on every finding; use UICrit (CC BY 4.0, with attribution) as a **static-screenshot regression set** for our critique quality (e.g., recall of human-critiqued regions, IoU of boxes), **never** as a source of holistic-score targets.

### 2.18 benjitaylor/agentation (visual annotation → agent feedback loop)

- **What it is:** A React toolbar (dev-only) that lets a human click any element of a running app and leave an annotation that carries rich machine context (selector path, bounding box, React component tree, source file, computed styles, accessibility info), plus an MCP/HTTP server (`agentation-mcp`, SQLite store, webhooks, SSE events) and two skills (setup; an autonomous self-driving critique mode). 4,833 stars — by far the most-used feedback-intake tool in this survey. Last push 2026-09-22.
- **URL:** https://github.com/benjitaylor/agentation
- **License:** `LICENSE` is headed PolyForm Shield License 1.0.0 (© 2026 Benji Taylor) and links to polyformproject.org, but its 27-line body is a short custom grant with a **non-compete clause** forbidding use of the software to provide a competing product or service; the canonical PolyForm Shield 1.0.0 (89 lines, sections Acceptance … Noncompete … Competition) also treats free products as competing (https://github.com/polyformproject/polyform-licenses/blob/master/PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0.md). Either reading forbids competing products, and UI-Evaluator's feedback-intake loop is arguably competing. **Ideas only; do not copy code, schema files or skill text.** Reading files that users export from Agentation (interoperability) should get a legal check first.
- **Files read:** `package/example/public/schema/annotation.v1.1.json` (and v1), `mcp/src/types.ts`, tool definitions in `mcp/src/server/mcp.ts` (lines ~130–290), `skills/agentation/SKILL.md`, `skills/agentation-self-driving/SKILL.md`, `skills/agentation-self-driving/references/two-session-workflow.md`, `LICENSE`.

**Annotation structure (described in my words, from `mcp/src/types.ts` and the v1.1 JSON schema).** Required: id, free-text comment, CSS element path, timestamp, x (percent of viewport width), y (px from document top, or from viewport when the element is fixed/sticky), element description. Optional page context: URL, bounding box, selected text, nearby text and elements, CSS classes, **computed styles**, **accessibility info**, full DOM path, attributes, iframe path, **React component hierarchy** and **source file** (from React debug info in dev). Classification: **intent** ∈ {fix, change, question, approve}; **severity** ∈ {blocking, important, suggestion}; **kind** ∈ {feedback, placement (drop a new component of type/size at a scroll position), rearrange (selector + original vs current rect)}. Lifecycle: **status** ∈ {pending, acknowledged, resolved, dismissed}, a **thread** of {role human|agent, content, timestamp}, `resolvedBy` human|agent, author id, created/updated/resolved times; sessions have status active/approved/closed. Event types: annotation created/updated/deleted, session created/updated/closed, thread message, action requested; webhooks forward them.

**MCP tool surface (9 tools):** list sessions; get session; get pending (per session); get all pending; **acknowledge**; **resolve** (optional summary of what was done); **dismiss** (reason *required*); **reply** (ask clarifying questions in the thread); **watch annotations** (blocks until new annotations arrive, then waits a batch window — default 10 s, max 60 s — and returns the batch; first-annotation timeout default 120 s, max 300 s). The watch tool's description encodes the loop contract: after addressing each item call resolve with a summary, resolve only what the user accepted, and leave rejected changes open.

**Workflow rules worth noting.**
- Setup skill: a visible toolbar and a healthy server do not prove that browser feedback actually reaches the agent — create one clearly identified test annotation and read back the *exact* comment and page through the MCP tool before claiming setup works; if any part cannot be verified, say which.
- Self-driving critic: always headed (the user watches), verify the toolbar exists, 5–8 annotations per page, 2–3 sentences each, specific and actionable with 1–2 concrete alternatives, name the principle, verify the annotation count increased after each submit.
- Two-session pattern: session 1 critiques, session 2 blocks on watch, greps the codebase by selector/component name, fixes, **runs checks and inspects the visual result before resolving**, and leaves failed or unverified work open.

**Strengths.** Captures the *exact element* plus code-locating context (component tree, source file, selector) at the moment the human points at it — the hardest part of mapping human feedback to a code change; explicit intent and severity fields; a clean lifecycle with human-gated resolution; batching for hands-free loops.

**Weaknesses.** Severity has only three levels and is author-assigned (no frequency/persistence); no notion of *evidence* beyond the element snapshot; feedback is from developers/designers, not end-users; non-compete license.

**Adopt (as ideas, own implementation).** Our feedback-item schema should capture: element locator (selector + bbox + component/source hint), page URL + viewport, verbatim comment, **intent** (fix/change/question/approve-equivalent), reporter severity *kept separate* from our computed severity, lifecycle status with **dismiss-requires-reason** and **resolve-requires-summary**, a human/agent thread, `resolvedBy`, and the rule that resolution is confirmed by the human, otherwise the item stays open. Also adopt the end-to-end intake self-test (round-trip one tagged test item before trusting the pipeline).

**marker-insights.** Read-only aggregation over a wide window: content/functional ratio, per-page hotspots, recurring themes with frequency and severity, trend by `createdAt` bucket, single root causes; action per theme (fix now, batch, or watch); **rank by impact × frequency**; sample-drill only per cluster.

**Operating rules (all skills).** Resolve before acting; discover enum values before writing; read cheaply first and drill into details only when they change a decision; **propose, then apply** — print a table (issue, field, old → new) and write only after confirmation; default internal comment visibility; never embed tokens.

**Strengths.** Practical, production triage logic; evidence-first bug localisation (console + network + screenshot); content-vs-functional split matches what real website feedback looks like; gated writes.

**Weaknesses.** Criticality scale is ad hoc (no frequency/persistence definitions); near-duplicate detection is left to judgement; no usability-heuristic framing (fine for bugs, weaker for UX complaints).

**Adopt.** Intake pipeline steps (normalize → classify content/functional/usability → group by page+theme → dedupe → criticality from impact and breadth with page-prominence weighting → quick wins → systemic root causes); rate from evidence, not the reporter's wording; repeat-reporter flag; bug-to-fix loop (localise with console/network/stack → root cause in two sentences or ask → minimal diff → verify with project tooling → write back with summary); propose-then-apply for any external write.

**Empirical-signal rules worth adopting (own words).**
- *Never dump unfiltered data* (`finding-sessions-to-watch`): turn an intent into one focused filter (reached page X; did event Y via a two-step SQL → session_ids; flag variant; device/viewport); exclude test accounts; window 7–30 days; order by a deliberate signal (activity, console errors, recency); return **3–5 recordings with a reason each**, plus the total behind the shortlist; discover the schema before filtering — confirm real event names/paths with the schema tool, never assume them.
- *Investigating one session* (`investigating-replay`): in parallel gather recording metadata, person, same-session event timeline (pageviews, autocapture, exceptions, rage clicks), linked error-tracking issues; synthesise **Who / What / Problems / Related issues / Context**; few events ⇒ a short session, to be noted rather than treated as breakage; optional LLM summary only after checking for an existing one, using a prompt that demands concreteness and no speculation.
- *Friction signal vs noise* (`signals-scout-session-replay`): "**Concentration-vs-diffusion** is the signal-vs-noise discriminator" — compare each surface with **its own history**, never an absolute bar. Concrete gates: a path is a friction-cluster candidate when its last-24 h rage clicks are **≥ ~3× its prior-13-day daily mean** with **≥ ~10 sessions and ≥ ~5 persons**; exclude single-person storms; first normalise against total traffic (if rage clicks rose with traffic, it is normal product variation); normalise URLs (strip query/fragment, replace numeric IDs with `:id`); corroborate with dead clicks, console-errors-after-click and quick-backs on the same sessions (upgrading annoyance to broken); broken-experience cohorts need ≥ 10 sessions and ≥ 5 users and a step-change vs the prior 13 days; a new page with no history is a pattern note, not a report; quantify on events (rage clicks fire whether or not a session was recorded), illustrate with recordings; capture-cliff rule: recording/traffic ratio < ~40% of its 14-day norm while traffic stays within ~25% (needs ≥ ~100 recordings/day for ≥ 7 days), check config/SDK changes before calling it an outage; SQL pitfalls (client clocks in the future → upper-bound windows).
- *Heatmaps* (`assessing-heatmaps`): heatmap data is geometry only; **name the hot elements by cross-referencing autocapture on the same URL** (element text/selector); segment by viewport width (desktop vs mobile folds); prefer unique visitors over total clicks; rank problems: rage-click clusters → clicks on non-interactive elements → key CTAs below the scroll cliff → ignored primary actions; the agent has no screenshot in context and must not claim to have looked at the page.
- *Surveys* (`creating-surveys`): 1–3 questions unless asked; rating + optional open question for NPS/CSAT; targeting rules must not be broadened silently; a saved survey configuration does not prove that an in-app popup rendered; zero responses right after launch do not prove failure.
- *Experiments* (`diagnosing-experiment-results`, MCP tools): covers sample-ratio mismatch, identity fragmentation, peeking, A/A significance, multiple comparisons, Bayesian vs frequentist; `experiment-calculate-running-time` estimates sample size and duration from metric type, baseline and MDE.
- *Interviews* (`planning-voice-agent-user-interviews`): recruit by behaviour from analytics — heavy users, **drop-offs** (≤ 2 uses, inactive > 14 days), at-risk (≥ 3 uses, inactive > 14 days); default balanced sample 5 heavy + 3 drop-off + 2 at-risk; open-ended, non-leading questions (ask what made someone decide to try a feature, not whether they liked it); audience snapshots and caps (500) require an explicit user decision.
- *Human calibration of AI observers* (MCP tools `vision-observations-label-create`, `vision-scanners-prompt-suggestions-generate`): humans rate each AI replay observation thumbs up/down with optional feedback; ratings drive a calibration chart and prompt-rewrite suggestions; the tool description says to "**record the person's verdict, never your own reading of the result**".

**MCP eval harness** (`services/mcp/evals/README.md`): a versioned benchmark of realistic intents (paraphrased from real usage, no PII), each with `expected_tools`, `acceptable_tools`, `success_criteria` (LLM judge), an optional deterministic read-only `probe`, and per-task seeded `fixtures` so runs are order-independent; scores = task success, tool-selection accuracy, schema failures, tool error rate, retries, latency p50/p95, tokens; a change counts as an improvement only if these scores show it.

**Adopt.** For empirical feedback intake: concentration-vs-own-baseline rule with minimum sessions/persons; URL normalisation; corroboration ladder (rage → dead click / error-after-click / quick-back); autocapture to name elements; shortlist-with-reasons for replays; behaviour-based interview recruiting with balanced segments; human thumbs-up/down calibration of any AI observer, recorded only from the human. For our own QA: PostHog-style task benchmark (intent, expected tools, success criteria, seeded fixtures, version pinning).

### 2.22 hashfunction/MarkuprPlus (narrated screen-capture feedback → per-mark Markdown issues)

- **What it is:** A Windows/macOS desktop app + CLI + MCP server + GitHub Action: the user records one window while talking, holds Ctrl/Cmd and circles problems on the live screen; on stop, Whisper transcribes **on-device**, narration is aligned to each mark, and a Markdown report is written with one finding per circled mark, each with its own annotated frame (IDs `MX-001`…). 79 stars, v3.2.0, last push 2026-10-01.
- **URL:** https://github.com/hashfunction/MarkuprPlus
- **License:** MIT (© 2026 Eddie San Juan) — adaptable with attribution.
- **Files read:** `README.md` (loop, privacy), `README-MCP.md` (9 tools), `examples/feedback-session-example.md`, `src/main/ai/analysisContract.ts` (system prompt + JSON schema + validator), head of `src/main/output/MarkedIssueReportBuilder.ts` (narration-to-mark alignment), list of `src/mcp/tools/` and `src/main/output/templates/` (GitHub issue, Jira, Linear, JSON, Markdown, HTML).

**Key content.**
- *Capture integrity/privacy:* records only the chosen window and **stops if the window identity becomes ambiguous** rather than widening capture; the app's own picker/HUD/canvas are excluded from the recording at OS level; local-first processing. If the analysis provider fails, the capture is preserved and a rule-based report is produced with the failure reason recorded.
- *Narration ↔ mark alignment* (`MarkedIssueReportBuilder.ts`): narration segments are matched to a mark using time windows (constants: 30 s comment look-back, 12 s preceding fallback) plus a token-overlap evidence score (stop-words removed; exact containment = 1.0; ≥ 2 shared meaningful tokens required).
- *Analysis contract* (`analysisContract.ts`): "Preserve the user's voice. Quote their exact words. Never rephrase"; group repeated mentions of one area into one item; reference screenshots by index; one concrete one-sentence action per item; priority Critical/High/Medium/Low derived from the narrator's described severity; exactly one category of **Bug / UX Issue / Performance / Suggestion / Question / Positive Note**; empty `items` when there is neither narration nor screenshots. Strict JSON schema (`additionalProperties: false`): item = `title, category, priority, quote, screenshotIndices, actionItem, area`; plus `summary, themes, positiveNotes, metadata{totalItems, criticalCount, highCount}`; counts are **recomputed by code**, unknown enums are coerced to safe defaults.
- *Report* (`examples/feedback-session-example.md`): per item `FB-00n` title, severity, type, **timestamp in the recording**, signals/keywords, verbatim *What happened* quote block, evidence screenshots, suggested next step; summary tables by type and severity. Exporters to GitHub issues, Linear, Jira.
- *MCP tools:* `capture_screenshot` (label, display; returns image ref + cursor/app/focused-element context), `capture_with_voice` (3–300 s), `start_recording` / `stop_recording`, `analyze_video`, `analyze_screenshot`, `describe_screen`, `push_to_github`, `push_to_linear`.

**Strengths.** Lowest-friction way for a *human* to give rich, time-stamped, element-pointed feedback; verbatim-quote rule; per-mark atomicity (three circles = three issues); robust structured-output validation; privacy-preserving capture.

**Weaknesses.** Priority comes from the narrator's described severity (not frequency across users); categories mix bug/UX/performance without heuristics; the example report shows a narrator asserting a button is below a 44 px minimum — a claim our pipeline would need to *measure*, not trust (44 px is Apple HIG/WCAG AAA; AA is 24 px).

**Adopt.** One mark/annotation = one feedback item; verbatim quote + recording timestamp + frame as mandatory evidence; category enum incl. `Positive Note` and `Question` (not everything is a defect); code-recomputed counts and schema-validated LLM output; treat numeric claims inside feedback as hypotheses to be verified by measurement.

## 3. Cross-source synthesis

### 3.1 Where the sources agree (consensus)

1. **Nielsen's 0–4 scale is the lingua franca for severity** (mastepanoski, 45ck, uw-ssec, EliaAlberti, carlsz, AndersonWang, UXCascade's issue detector, noodisD after its migration). Most also name Nielsen's three factors (frequency, impact, persistence). Several add a useful convention: **0 is a non-finding** — omitted from counts (carlsz) or reserved for *false positives* shown separately (noodisD).
2. **A finding is a structured record, not prose.** Fields that recur across almost every source: ID; violated rule (heuristic number, WCAG SC, CW question, or journey step) — often primary plus corroborating; location (screen/route, component, state; increasingly `file:line`, selector and bounding box); evidence (screenshot path, measurement, trace step, DOM/a11y fact, console/network entry, verbatim quote with timestamp); description; why it matters / impact on the stated goal; severity with justification; specific recommendation; effort; positives kept separately.
3. **Evidence discipline is the main quality lever.** The most mature sources converge on: no claim without observable evidence (EliaAlberti, carlsz, noodisD, UXBench); an explicit list of what could *not* be assessed (EliaAlberti's not-assessable list, carlsz's coverage / not-inspected appendix); N/A instead of guessed low scores (arely, RUXAILAB); missing evidence must not be scored as failure (arely) and, conversely, a claim that something is absent needs positive evidence of its absence (noodisD).
4. **Cognitive walkthrough = Wharton's four questions per atomic action, from a defined persona and start state, for a defined task with success criteria.** All CW implementations keep the four questions (some paraphrase), most add a per-step record of visible cues and likely action, and the better ones add failure stories (uw-ssec) and Spencer-style credible-story framing.
5. **Where multiple evaluators exist, independence + merge is the agreed shape** (NN/g; arely implements it; uw-ssec specifies merge = dedupe + mean severity and calibration when ratings differ by more than one point; RUXAILAB reports mean/SD/range across evaluators and keeps AI agents as separately labelled evaluators).
6. **Humans stay in the loop for consequential writes and for closing items**: propose-then-apply tables (marker), resolve only what the human accepted (agentation), agree/disagree export (averliz), thumbs-up/down calibration recorded only from the human (PostHog vision labels).
7. **Synthetic users and LLM judges are complements, not substitutes** — every research prototype says this (UXAgent system paper, UXCascade, UXBench, MLLM-Usability), and the one production fork that measured it (noodisD) found most raw agent findings false until gated.

### 3.3 How CW tasks are derived across sources, and a merged rule

Sources derive tasks from: the user's request or a default of 5–7 key tasks (mastepanoski Nielsen), 3–5 key tasks (Norman skill, uw-ssec command), 3–7 journeys prioritised as first-time, core, edit/undo, failure recovery, returning (45ck journey-map), an interview that produces critical-user-journey contracts with criticality (carlsz spec-cuj), the PR diff's changed routes (averliz), persona × goal matrices (UXCascade), recorded tasks per persona (MLLM-Usability), real usage intents (PostHog evals) and analytics drop-off cohorts (PostHog interview recruiting). **Merged rule:** derive candidate tasks from (1) declared CUJs, (2) funnels/drop-off steps and top flows in analytics, (3) feedback clusters, (4) routes touched by the change; rank by criticality × frequency; write each as goal + scenario (no UI words) + start state + observable success condition + avoided hints + correct action sequence.

### 3.4 Multi-evaluator merge — what the sources together specify

1. Run N = 3 (2–5) independent evaluators over one frozen evidence bundle (arely; NN/g). 2. Each produces findings with locator, evidence and factor notes; no severity yet (our resolution of C2). 3. Merge: deterministic-key dedupe, keep all labels and evidence (C5). 4. Rate: ≥3 raters score each merged problem 0–4 blind; final = mean (NN/g; uw-ssec); also record *how many evaluators detected it* (that count is itself a confidence signal and, with overlaps, allows a capture–recapture estimate of undiscovered problems — my proposal, using the Nielsen–Landauer model in §2.0). 5. Disagreement report for spread ≥ 2, worst first, with each rater's note in their own voice (arely); agreement → fix, disagreement → test with users. 6. Optional debrief recalculation, keeping pre/post values (NN/g debrief; uw-ssec calibrate). 7. Expect low overlap: Hertzum & Jacobsen report 5–65% any-two agreement, so low agreement is normal and should route to empirical validation rather than be settled by fiat.

### 3.5 Gaps no source fills

- **No skill implements Nielsen's full protocol** (≥ 2 passes, 3–5 independent evaluators, post-hoc averaged severity, debrief). arely is closest on independence; uw-ssec specifies merge/calibration in text only.
- **Audit and feedback never meet.** Feedback tools (agentation, Marker, Formbricks, PostHog, MarkuprPlus) do not produce heuristic-coded findings, and audit skills do not ingest feedback as evidence (except mastepanoski's untrusted `known_issues` input). UI-Evaluator should link each feedback cluster to findings, raising frequency and confidence, or creating candidates that must be reproduced.
- **Fix verification is thin.** Only UXCascade (single-step action change), carlsz (journey replay), marker (project tooling) and agentation's fixer session (run checks, inspect the visual result before resolving) verify fixes; nobody re-runs the *originating check* plus the affected journeys and also checks for regressions.
- **No precision/recall measurement of evaluators against ground truth** in any shipped tool; noodisD plans it; UXBench measures repair lift; UICrit offers static critiques. We need our own seeded-defect suite.
- **Empirical study design is shallow** — only uw-ssec (A/B), intent (HEART/GSM/triangulation) and PostHog (experiment diagnosis, interviews) address objectives → tasks → data → analysis, and none states correlation ≠ causation as a rule for reading post-release analytics.

## 5. Open questions and things to verify later

1. **License clarifications before any reuse beyond ideas:** (a) uw-ssec `uiux-design-team` — repo is BSD-3-Clause but the plugin declares itself proprietary to BitBarrel LLC; (b) repos with no LICENSE file: Jackwwj619/UXBench (README claims MIT), neuhai/UXAgent (README badge MIT), ruxailab/ai-heuristic-evaluation, PostHog/ai-plugin; (c) benjitaylor/agentation non-compete — get legal advice before even reading Agentation-exported files for interoperability; (d) Formbricks AGPLv3 — integrate via API/MCP only.
2. **UXBench details to check in the paper appendix:** whether the fixed scorer (GPT-5.4-Mini, also one of the judges) creates self-preference bias; how the code's 1–10 judge scale maps to the paper's 1–5 rubric; inter-rater statistics of the six-person human study.
3. **MLLM-Usability (FSE 2026):** compare my computed means from the shipped CSVs (clarity 4.21, plausibility 4.04, rec. plausibility 4.10; 137/94/54 on the would-have-detected-it-myself item) with the paper's reported figures and participant count.
4. **UICrit reliability:** my ICC(1) ≈ 0.03–0.05 for holistic ratings is from the public 3-annotator CSV; confirm annotator assignment (same pool? different anchors?) and whether the paper reports its own agreement before citing this externally.
5. **Peeking inflation:** my simulation (10 equally spaced looks, α = 0.05) gave ≈ 19%; confirm against Armitage, McPherson & Rowe (1969) table before quoting a published number.
6. **Severity table calibration:** the suggested-severity grid (EVAL-012) and the disagreement threshold (spread ≥ 2) should be validated on our own runs against human raters (weighted κ), and adjusted.
7. **Evaluator count vs cost:** estimate λ (share of problems found by one evaluator agent) from our own runs and use ProblemsFound(i) = N·(1 − (1 − λ)^i) or capture–recapture on evaluator overlaps to choose N; Nielsen's human λ (19–51%, mean 34%) may not transfer to LLM evaluators, which may be more correlated with each other (lower effective diversity).
8. **Ease-of-fix field:** the course's HE report template lists ease-of-fix separately from severity; confirm the course definition and whether to keep S/M/L effort or a separate 0–4 ease scale.
9. **CW wording:** confirm whether the course expects Wharton's four questions verbatim or Spencer's two-question streamlined variant as an allowed shortcut.
10. **Thresholds borrowed from single sources** (averliz 4 px spacing / 5 px shift / 2 px alignment; UXBench coverage percentages; PostHog 3× / 10 sessions / 5 people; noodisD 80-char / 20 KB perception-artefact heuristic) are engineering defaults, not standards — keep them configurable and log them in every report.
11. **Claims I could not verify:** mastepanoski's statement that WCAG 2.2 was approved as ISO/IEC 40500:2025 (W3C press-release link given in the skill, not opened); its claim that automated accessibility tools catch roughly 30–40% of issues (varies by study and by counting method); uw-ssec's efficiency-ratio (0.7/0.4) and error-rate (5%/15%) benchmarks; UX-researcher sample-size claims (8–12 per segment); RUXAILAB AHP README's Saaty scale description (checked only against secondary sources).
12. **Related work not in scope but worth a later look:** PerceptUI (arXiv:2606.05697, human-aligned synthetic users), Agent A/B (arXiv:2504.09723, agent-run A/B tests), Avenir-UX (arXiv:2604.09581) — surfaced by searches, not read.
